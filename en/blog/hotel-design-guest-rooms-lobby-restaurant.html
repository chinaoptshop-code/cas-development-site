Hotel design: how to plan the guest rooms, lobby and restaurant

CAS Development · 23 November 2026

In a hotel, design is counted in money: how many minutes it takes to clean a room, how many years the corridor flooring will last, whether someone from the street will walk into the restaurant. Here is how hotel design works, from the standard room to the lobby and restaurant, and what should be decided before a style is chosen.

Where does hotel design begin?

Hotel design begins with the guest: who they are and how long they stay. A business traveller for one night, a family for a week and a couple for a weekend expect different things from a room: a desk, a kitchenette or a bathtub with a view. The answer determines the room types, the area of the public spaces and the budget per room. Style is chosen after that. How we run such projects is described on the hotel design and turnkey fit-out page.

The brief for the designer should state:

  • the format: city hotel, boutique hotel, aparthotel, country resort
  • the number of rooms and their types
  • who will operate the hotel and what they require of back-of-house areas
  • whether there will be a restaurant, bar, spa or conference hall
  • the budget and the opening date

How do you design a standard room for the whole room stock?

A room is designed as a product that is then repeated dozens of times. Furniture, lighting, sanitaryware and textiles are identical in all rooms of one type: this makes them easier to buy in a batch, repair and replace. The layout is standard too: an entrance area with a wardrobe and a place for a suitcase, a bathroom next to the service shaft, a bedroom with a workspace by the window.

A guest judges a room by details that are often missed in a residential interior:

  • sockets on both sides of the bed and at the desk
  • a master light switch at the entrance and by the bed
  • curtains that black out the room completely
  • doors and walls that keep out corridor noise
  • a shelf in the shower, hooks and a place for wet towels
  • a luggage rack, so the suitcase does not end up on the bed

Hotel design and turnkey fit-out

Why build a mock-up room before the roll-out?

A mock-up room reveals mistakes before they are repeated in every room, so before ordering furniture for the entire hotel, it is worth completing one room in full: finishes, furniture, lighting and textiles. It is used to test what a rendering does not show. Is it easy for a housekeeper to change the linen and clean under the bed, do the wardrobe doors hit the entrance door, how does the lighting look in the evening, are there enough hooks.

Corrections are made to the drawings and the specification, and only then are the remaining rooms started. A mistake in one room costs little; repeated in every room, it is multiplied across the whole room stock.

Which materials stand up to hotel use?

A hotel needs materials that clean easily and wear slowly, because a hotel room wears out faster than an apartment: suitcases, housekeeping trolleys, frequent cleaning. So materials are chosen by how they age and how they clean.

  • floors: hard-wearing coverings in rooms and corridors, with no thresholds in the trolley’s path
  • walls: washable paint, corner protection and protection around the luggage rack
  • furniture: durable edges and hardware, upholstery that can be cleaned or replaced
  • bathroom: large-format tiles with a minimum of joints, wall-hung sanitaryware, a floor drain
  • textiles: fabrics that withstand frequent washing

Furniture, lighting and textiles for standard rooms are best ordered in a batch directly from factories in China, including made-to-order pieces by size, colour and material. Delivery takes 35–70 days, so the specification is agreed early. A list of items is given in the article on hotel furniture procurement.

How do you plan the lobby and reception?

The lobby is planned both as the guest’s first impression and as a workplace for staff. The reception desk is positioned so that the receptionist can see the entrance and the lifts, and the guest understands at once where to go. Nearby you need luggage storage, a waiting area and a back office behind the desk.

Guest and staff routes are kept apart: linen, waste and food deliveries should not pass through the lobby. In a small hotel the lobby is often combined with a bar or café, and then the space works all day, not only at check-in time.

How do you plan a hotel restaurant and breakfasts?

A hotel restaurant is planned for two audiences: guests at breakfast and visitors from the city in the evening. The latter need a separate street entrance or an obvious route through the lobby; otherwise the dining room will stand empty after breakfast.

The restaurant’s kitchen, ventilation and drainage are designed together with the hotel: fitting an extract system and a grease trap into a finished building is far harder. That is the subject of the article on restaurant building services. An example of a venue where we did both the design and the fit-out is the Monpark restaurant in central Kyiv.

Questions and answers

How much does hotel design cost?

A design project costs from $30/m², with a minimum project fee of $2,500. Renovation and procurement are priced from the design after the brief and the site survey.

What is a mock-up room?

It is one room completed in full before furniture and materials are ordered for the whole hotel. It is used to check ease of cleaning, lighting, furniture and details, and to correct the specification.

Where should hotel design start: with the rooms or the lobby?

With the format and the room stock: who the guest is, how many rooms and of what types. The lobby, restaurant and style are designed after that.

Do you work with hotels outside Kyiv?

Yes, across Ukraine and in Europe. Prices in Europe are higher: design from $45/m², minimum $4,000.
