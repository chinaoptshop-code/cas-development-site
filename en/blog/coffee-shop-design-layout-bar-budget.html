Coffee shop design: layout, bar and budget for a small venue

CAS Development · 18 November 2026

In a small coffee shop every metre has a job: the same floor area has to hold the bar, the queue, the display case and seats where a guest will want to stay a little longer. Here is where coffee shop design starts, how to plan the bar and the seating area, which services to design before the fit-out and what the project costs.

Where does coffee shop design start?

Format first, style second. Takeaway coffee, coffee with desserts and seating, a bakery café or a place that serves breakfast — each means a different bar length, different equipment, a different number of seats and entirely different building services. If the format changes halfway through the fit-out, the part you have to redo is the most expensive one: water supply, drainage and electrics.

Before you meet the designer, it helps to have answers to a few questions:

  • what the format and menu are: drinks only, drinks and desserts, or a kitchen as well
  • which equipment is already chosen or bought: espresso machine, grinders, refrigerated display cases, oven
  • how many seats you need and how long guests usually stay
  • when the peak hours are and how many people stand in the queue
  • the budget and the opening date you are aiming for

Why is the bar the heart of a coffee shop layout?

The bar sets the rhythm of the whole venue. A guest walks in, sees the menu and the display case, orders, pays, waits and collects the drink. When these steps follow a loop, the queue does not block the entrance and people carrying drinks do not bump into those who have just come in. That is why we usually move the pick-up point away from the till and put the dessert display where the guest can see it while waiting.

Behind the counter there is a logic of its own: espresso machine, grinders, sink, under-counter fridges, ice, a stock of cups. The barista should work without extra steps, and at peak hours there are often two people behind the bar. The espresso machine needs water, a filter, a drain and a dedicated electrical circuit. All of this goes into the drawings before the fit-out, because moving water and drainage after the finishes are done is expensive.

How many seats, and what kind, does the room need?

One coffee shop serves very different guests: someone drops in for five minutes, someone works on a laptop for half a day, someone comes with a child and a pushchair. It is good when the room offers a choice:

  • high seats by the window for a quick coffee
  • small tables for two that are easy to push together for a group
  • a few soft seats for long conversations
  • a large shared table with sockets for people who work
  • space for a pushchair and a clear route for staff

Think separately about the guest toilet, the back room and storage for supplies. On the plan they are often the last thing to be squeezed in, and then boxes of milk end up in the seating area. If there is a chance to add a terrace, draw it from the start as well: it affects the entrance and where the furniture will be kept in winter.

How do light, materials and acoustics set the mood?

Lighting creates half the mood in a coffee shop. General light should be warm and even, accent light goes above the bar and the display case so that desserts look appetising, and pendants above the tables hang at a height where they do not dazzle. For the evening it is useful to have a separate scene with the general light dimmed.

Materials have to withstand daily cleaning and a steady flow of people. The bar top, the floor by the entrance and by the counter, furniture fronts, upholstery — we choose all of them with an eye to how they will look in a year. Another common problem is noise. Concrete, glass and tiles reflect sound, and in a full room it becomes hard to talk. Soft furniture, textiles and acoustic panels on the ceiling or walls reduce the noise noticeably.

Do not forget the facade. People choose a coffee shop from the street: they see the window, the entrance, the sign and the guests inside. The entrance is as much a part of the design as the room itself.

How much does coffee shop design cost?

A design project for a venue at CAS costs from $30/m², and the minimum project fee is $2,500. For a small coffee shop it is the minimum that applies: for example, 60 m² × $30 = $1,800, so the project will cost $2,500. For a 120 m² venue the guide figure is 120 m² × $30 = $3,600.

The project includes the concept, the layout of the bar, seating area and back-of-house rooms, 3D visualisations, working drawings, lighting and electrical plans, and a specification of materials and furniture. The fit-out is priced separately, by the project estimate: the amount depends on the condition of the premises, the ventilation and the number of water and drainage points. If CAS carries out the fit-out to our design, the final total does not deviate from the estimate by more than ±5%. More about the service is on the restaurant, café and bar design page.

Which coffee shop mistakes are expensive to fix?

  • the equipment was bought before the project, and it does not fit the bar or needs a different power supply
  • the till and the pick-up point are in the same place, so the queue blocks the entrance
  • there is no extraction above the oven, and the smell of baking hangs in the room all day
  • too few sockets by the tables, or they are where nobody sits
  • “residential” materials that wear and darken within six months
  • storage and a staff area were not planned at all

Most of these mistakes are visible on the plan. That is why we show the venue in 3D before the fit-out and walk through the whole route of the guest and the barista with the owner. Questions and answers

How much does a coffee shop design project cost?

From $30/m², with a minimum project fee of $2,500. For a 60 m² coffee shop the minimum applies, that is $2,500. We give the exact amount after the brief and a visit to the premises.

What does coffee shop design include?

The concept, the layout of the bar, seating area and back rooms, 3D visualisations, working drawings, lighting and electrical plans, a specification of materials and furniture, and a fit-out estimate.

Is it better to buy equipment before or after the project?

It is best to choose the equipment models before the bar is planned and to buy them after the drawings are approved. That way the dimensions, water, drainage and electrics will match what actually stands behind the counter.

Can you do the fit-out and furniture for a coffee shop?

Yes. Our own team carries out the fit-out, and we can order furniture, lighting and decor direct from factories in China. If CAS does the fit-out, the final total stays within ±5% of the estimate.
