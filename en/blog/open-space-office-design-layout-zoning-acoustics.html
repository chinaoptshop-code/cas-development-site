Open-space office design: layout, zoning and acoustics

CAS Development · 16 November 2026

An open office is more flexible than cellular offices, but it only works when zones, walkways, light and sound are thought through. We look at how to approach open-space office design: where to start, how to zone the space and what to do about noise at the design stage.

Where to start: the team and work scenarios

Before drawing desks, understand how the team works. How many people are in the office every day and how many come once a week. Who is constantly on calls and who needs quiet. How often meetings happen and for how many people. Whether clients visit.

The answers decide whether an open space suits you at all. If half the team is phone sales and the other half accounting or development, one big hall without zones will be a source of constant conflict. We compared the formats in the article open space or private offices.

  • number of workstations now and in a year or two
  • the share of people on calls and those who need to concentrate
  • number and size of meeting rooms
  • an area for receiving clients
  • kitchen, rest area, storage, printers

Planning an open-space office

Planning starts with what cannot be changed: windows, columns, risers, ventilation shafts, emergency exits. Workstations go closer to daylight, while meeting rooms, server rooms and storage go deeper into the space where there is less light.

Next come the walkways. The main route from the entrance to the kitchen and toilets should not pass the desks of those working in quiet. Leave enough room between rows of desks to push back a chair and walk by without bumping a colleague.

It helps to build in a reserve: a few free places or a hot-desk zone that can easily become permanent workstations when the team grows.

Zoning without solid walls

The main idea of open space is that the space is not divided into rooms but does not remain one hall either. Zones are defined by furniture, plants, light, floor finishes and wall colour. Each zone has its function:

  • a quiet zone for concentration — away from the entrance and kitchen
  • team desks for those who talk a lot
  • focus rooms or single-person booths for calls
  • meeting rooms of different sizes — glazed so as not to steal light
  • a kitchen-café and rest area
  • a reception or guest meeting area

At Office Chicago we zoned the space without solid walls: green walls and islands of live plants separate work groups, and the kitchen-café with a white island became a place where the team gathers informally. The space stayed bright and open, yet every zone reads clearly.

Acoustics in an open space

Noise is the main complaint about open offices. Bare walls, a concrete ceiling, glass and hard floors reflect sound, and a conversation at one end of the hall can be heard at the other. This has to be solved in the design, while materials can still be specified and zones placed correctly.

  • sound-absorbing ceiling panels or suspended elements — the largest area for absorption
  • wall panels opposite work groups and next to meeting rooms
  • carpet tiles in work and rest areas
  • upholstered furniture, textiles, curtains
  • green walls and tall shelving as sound diffusers
  • noisy zones — kitchen, reception, printers — away from quiet ones

Lighting, sockets and services

General diffused light is complemented by task lighting over desks and accent lighting in rest areas. Track systems are handy because fittings can be moved when the layout changes.

Sockets and network points should be under every desk, not along the walls with extension leads running across the walkway. For island desks, floor outlets or cable trays in the furniture are provided. Air conditioners and ventilation are positioned so that the airflow does not blow directly onto workstations.

What to check in the design before renovation

  • a layout plan with all workstations and room for growth
  • a diagram of zones and routes
  • a ceiling plan with acoustic panels
  • a floor plan with different finishes by zone
  • an electrical and network plan with outlets for every desk
  • a lighting plan with groups
  • a furniture specification and an estimate

Questions and answers

How much does open-space office design cost?

From $30/m², with a minimum project price of $2,500. For a 200 m² office that is from $6,000.

How do you reduce noise in an open office?

Build sound-absorbing ceilings and wall panels, carpet tiles, upholstered furniture and plants into the design, and place noisy zones — kitchen, reception, printers — away from quiet workstations.

Can an open space be zoned without partitions?

Yes. Zones are defined by furniture, green walls, light, floor finishes and colour. Focus rooms and glazed meeting rooms are added for calls and meetings.

Do you renovate offices to an existing design?

Yes. Office renovation starts from $350/m² (works and rough materials); the exact sum comes from the estimate based on the design.
