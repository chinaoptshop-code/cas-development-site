Reconstruct an old house or build a new one: which pays off

CAS Development · 1 November 2026

Reconstructing an old house makes sense when the foundation and walls are sound, while a new house pays off when the structures are weak — a decision owners of a dacha, a family home or a bought house with a history all face. Emotions pull towards keeping it, the calculator sometimes towards demolition. We look at how to decide on facts: what to survey, when reconstruction makes sense and what both budgets consist of.

Where to start: reconstruction or a new house?

Start not with “which is cheaper” but with which option gives you the house you want to live in, for reasonable money and without surprises. Sometimes an old house has a solid base, a good position on the plot and a character worth keeping. Sometimes fresh plaster hides a weak foundation, rotten floors and a layout that cannot be saved without full demolition.

The answer comes not from an impression on a visit but from a survey and a comparison of two scenarios against the same list of costs. Below is how to do it step by step: from assessing the structures to comparing estimates, documents and the risks of each option.

How does reconstruction differ from renovation?

Renovation refreshes what is there: finishes, services, windows. House reconstruction changes the house itself: its structure, volume or the use of rooms. Typical reconstruction works:

  • strengthening the foundation and load-bearing walls
  • replacing floors and the roof, building an attic floor
  • an extension or an extra storey, changing the volume of the house
  • re-planning with relocation of load-bearing elements to the design
  • insulating the facade, replacing windows, a new facade
  • complete replacement of services: heating, water, drainage, electrics, ventilation

Reconstruction always needs a design with a structural section. Load-bearing elements cannot be changed or a storey added without calculations: the load on the foundation and walls grows, and a mistake here costs far more than any saving.

Do you need a house survey before deciding?

Yes, there is no decision without a survey: before comparing estimates you need to know the condition of the house. The survey is carried out by engineers: they assess the structures, record defects and conclude what can be kept, what must be strengthened and what replaced. A survey is especially important for houses that stood unheated for a long time, went through several owners and unregistered “add-ons”, or have timber floors.

  • foundation: type, depth, cracks, settlement, humidity of the basement or plinth
  • walls: material, cracks, deviation from vertical, signs of damp
  • floors: material, deflection, condition of timber beams, signs of rot
  • roof: rafter system, covering, leaks, insulation
  • services: age and condition of networks, possibility of connecting more capacity
  • documents: technical passport, ownership, whether the actual house matches the documents

The survey result is the basis for both scenarios. If the structures are in good condition, reconstruction usually makes sense. If the foundation and walls are a problem, the comparison almost always leans towards new construction.

What you get after the survey

A good report is not a general phrase like “the house is in normal condition” but a list of structures with their condition, photos of defects and recommendations: what to keep, what to strengthen, what to replace. With such a document an architect can design the reconstruction and an estimator can calculate quantities. Without it both work blind, and the gap between estimate and reality appears already during demolition.

When does reconstructing an old house pay off?

  • the foundation and walls are in satisfactory condition and will carry the new loads
  • the house sits well on the plot, and new construction in the same place is limited by setbacks
  • the house has architectural value or matters to the family
  • a moderate change is needed: an attic floor, a terrace or kitchen extension, a new roof
  • you want to keep part of the budget for the interior and landscaping

In this case reconstruction gives you a modern house while keeping the base. But the budget must include a reserve for hidden defects that appear during demolition. The size of the reserve depends on the survey findings.

Attic floor, extension, new roof

The most common reconstruction scenarios are converting a cold loft into an attic floor, extending with a terrace, kitchen-living room or garage, and replacing the roof with insulation. All of them change the loads and the junctions with the old walls. So an extension is designed with its own foundation and a well-thought-out movement joint, and an attic floor with a check of the floor and rafter system. If these junctions are solved correctly, the old and new volumes work as one house, without cracks at the joint.

When is it better to demolish and build a new house?

  • the foundation is weak or has settled, the walls have through cracks
  • floors and roof need complete replacement
  • the layout does not fit at all, and changes affect most load-bearing elements
  • the house is small, and an extension equals a new house in volume
  • you want modern energy efficiency that is hard to achieve within old walls
  • the documents do not match the actual house, and approving a reconstruction is harder than new construction

A new house gives full freedom of layout, modern structures and services from the start, and a more predictable estimate. Demolition of the old house, removal and disposal of waste must be added to the budget as a separate item. More on the stages of new construction on the turnkey house construction page.

If you decide to build new, use the experience of the old house: you already know where the sun is on the plot, where the wind comes from, where water stands after rain and which rooms were awkward. These observations are valuable input for house design, which starts from the plot, not from a picture of the facade.

Comparison: a decision checklist

  • Condition of structures: reconstruction — only with a good foundation and walls; new house — regardless of the old one's condition
  • Layout freedom: reconstruction — limited by load-bearing elements; new — full
  • Budget predictability: reconstruction — needs a reserve for hidden defects; new — a more accurate estimate
  • Energy efficiency: reconstruction — depends on walls and insulation; new — built into the design
  • Keeping the character: reconstruction — yes; new — only in architectural references
  • Extra costs: reconstruction — strengthening, survey; new — demolition and disposal

If reconstruction wins on most points, move in its direction. If the result is mixed, two questions decide: how important it is for you to keep this particular house, and how comfortable you are living with a budget reserve for unknown defects.

How do you compare the budgets of reconstruction and a new build?

Only estimates drawn up on the same logic can be compared: the same layout, the same area, the same level of finishes and services. Otherwise one option will look cheaper only because something is missing from it.

A reserve for hidden defects

Even after a good survey, some problems show up only during demolition: a rotten beam under the floor, a void in the masonry, old wiring in an unexpected place. So a reserve should be built into the reconstruction estimate, and it should be agreed in advance with the contractor how additional works are documented. A new house needs a smaller reserve, because all structures are new and described in the design.

Documents and approvals

Before the design, check the documents: ownership of the house and plot, the designated land use, whether the actual house matches the technical passport. Extensions and extra storeys built earlier without registration can complicate reconstruction. The approval procedure for reconstruction and new construction differs, so it is worth clarifying with specialists at the start so as not to rebuild the schedule midway.

What mistakes do owners of old houses make?

  • starting reconstruction without a survey “because the house is solid”
  • planning an extra storey without checking the foundation
  • comparing a full estimate for a new house with an incomplete reconstruction estimate
  • leaving no reserve for hidden defects in old structures
  • not checking the house and plot documents before the design

Questions and answers

Which pays off more — reconstruction or a new house?

It depends on the condition of the foundation, walls and floors. If the structures are in good condition, reconstruction is often justified; if not, a new house gives a more predictable budget and freedom of layout.

Can an extra storey be added to an old house?

Only after a survey and calculation: the foundation and walls must carry the new load. Sometimes strengthening is needed, which changes the economics of the project.

How much does a house reconstruction cost?

The cost is set after the survey and design, because it depends on the condition of the structures and the scope of changes. For comparison: new construction at CAS starts from $600/m², turnkey from $1,100/m².

Does reconstruction need a design?

Yes. Reconstruction changes the structure or volume of the house, so a design with a structural section and calculations is required.

Can you live in the house during reconstruction?

Not during work on structures, the roof and services: it is unsafe and gets in the way of the works. Plan other housing for this period in advance.
