Rough works and finishing works: what is the difference

CAS Development · 25 October 2026

Rough works in an apartment are everything that later disappears under the finishes: walls, floors, building services. Finishing works are what you see and touch every day. Here is how they differ, what each stage includes and why it is easier to give both to one contractor.

Rough and finishing works: the difference in short

The rough stage creates the “skeleton” of the apartment: the geometry of walls and floors and the concealed building services. The finishing stage dresses this skeleton in finishes: paint, tiles, flooring, doors, lighting, sanitary ware. Rough works are almost invisible in the finished interior, but they determine how long and how reliably everything will work. A mistake in the finishes shows immediately; a mistake in the rough works shows years later, when it can no longer be reached without opening up the walls.

What rough works in an apartment include

The scope depends on the condition of the apartment and on the design, but rough works in an apartment usually cover the following:

  • demolition of old finishes and partitions, waste removal
  • new partitions according to the re-planning layout
  • wall levelling: plastering on guide beads
  • floor screed, waterproofing of wet areas
  • electrics: chasing, cables, back boxes, distribution board
  • water and drainage: pipework, concealed frames, the hidden part of the plumbing
  • ventilation and routes for air conditioning
  • rough ceiling: framework, plasterboard, recesses for concealed lighting

This is the dirtiest and noisiest part of a renovation, but it is where everything is laid down that can no longer be changed without destroying the finished surfaces.

What finishing works include

Finishing works are the finishes that shape how the rooms look and how comfortable they are to use every day:

  • skimming walls and ceilings for painting
  • painting or wallpapering
  • tiling and grouting in the bathroom and kitchen
  • flooring: parquet, laminate, tiles or other as per the design
  • interior doors and architraves
  • sanitary ware: toilet, basin, taps, shower area
  • sockets, switches and light fittings
  • skirting boards and trim profiles

Order matters in finishing works too: first whatever creates dust, then painting, and at the very end the installation of lighting, sanitary ware and doors. The floor is protected from damage throughout.

What happens between the rough and finishing stages

Between the two stages there is a short but important stop — the pre-finishing inspection. The crew and the manager walk through the apartment with the design and check that everything is ready for finishing:

  • walls and floors are level — correcting deviations now is easier than after tiling
  • the positions of sockets, water outlets and lights match the drawings
  • waterproofing of wet areas is done and tested
  • screed and plaster have dried enough to be covered with finishes

Concealed works — electrics, pipes, waterproofing — are recorded separately. They are photographed before closing and signed off in a report, so that later it is clear where everything runs and who is responsible for what.

Why you cannot “save on the rough works”

The temptation is familiar: rough works are invisible, so why not go cheaper and faster. In reality, this is exactly where savings turn into the biggest costs:

  • uneven walls and floors — tiles do not sit right, the joints of made-to-measure furniture do not line up
  • poor waterproofing — leaks to the neighbours and relaying a finished bathroom
  • weak wiring or poor connections — a safety risk and opening up the walls
  • wrong routing of services — you cannot add a socket or move a light without chasing

Finishing materials can be replaced even several years later. Rough works cannot, which is why they should be done carefully, according to the design and with the concealed works recorded.

Furniture and appliances are delivered after the finishing works, once painting is finished, the flooring is laid and the apartment has been cleaned. Made-to-measure kitchens and wardrobes are measured against the finished walls — then their dimensions match the real geometry. Questions and answers

What are rough works in an apartment renovation?

This is the stage that creates the base of the rooms: demolition, partitions, plastering, screed, electrics, water and drainage, ventilation and the rough ceiling. There are no finishes yet at this stage.

What do finishing works include?

Skimming, painting or wallpaper, tiles, flooring, doors, sanitary ware, lighting and skirting boards — everything that shapes the look of the apartment and makes it ready to live in.

Can rough and finishing works be done by different contractors?

They can, but then responsibility is split between two teams, and claims about geometry or concealed works are harder to resolve. One contractor for both stages is simpler: a single estimate, manager and warranty.

When should furniture be delivered: before or after the finishing works?

After. Made-to-measure furniture is measured against the finished walls, and appliances and upholstered furniture are delivered once painting, flooring and cleaning are complete.
