A lawn for your plot: turf or seeded

CAS Development · 18 November 2026

Turf or seed is a question almost every homeowner asks. We compare both options, explain what the choice depends on and why the result of either depends more on preparing the base than on the lawn itself.

How does turf differ from a seeded lawn?

Turf is grown on special fields, cut together with a thin layer of soil and delivered to the plot in rolls. A seeded lawn is a grass seed mix sown straight onto the prepared base. Both give a good lawn; the difference is in speed, cost and how demanding the start is.

  • turf is green immediately after laying, seed takes a few weeks
  • turf costs more, seed is more economical over large areas
  • turf is less vulnerable to weeds at the start, seed needs weeding in the first season
  • seed offers a wider choice of mixes: shade-tolerant, sports, for dry spots
  • turf must be laid on the day of delivery, seed can wait for the right weather

When is turf the better choice?

Turf wins when you need a result straight away: handing over the house, a celebration, children and a dog who need grass this season. It also works well on slopes, where rain washes seed away, and on small areas by the terrace and pool, where looks matter from day one.

The downside is the price and the laying conditions. Rolls cannot stay rolled up for long, so the base must be ready when the lorry arrives, and for the first weeks the lawn is watered generously until it roots.

When is a seeded lawn enough?

A seeded lawn makes sense for large areas, the far parts of the plot and places where you can wait. It is easier to match to conditions: shade under trees, a sunny slope, a heavily used area. The best time to sow is spring or late summer and early autumn, when it is warm but not hot.

In its first season a seeded lawn needs attention: regular watering, the first cut at the right moment and weeding until the grass thickens.

Why does the base matter more than the type of lawn?

The most common reason for a poor lawn is neither the variety nor the turf but the base. If there is building rubble, undrained clay or uneven ground under the grass, problems will appear with either option.

  1. Clear building rubble and weed roots.
  2. Set the falls and deal with water — drainage if needed.
  3. Lay irrigation and lighting cables.
  4. Bring in and level topsoil, then roll it.
  5. Lay the turf or sow the seed.
  6. Set up irrigation and do the first cut on time.

The order of works is clearly visible in the “Landscape from scratch” animation on the landscape design page: the lawn appears only after the services and paving.

Where does a lawn struggle or not belong?

Grass does not grow equally well everywhere. Under dense trees and on the north side of the house it lacks light, on steep slopes rain washes it away, and in narrow passages along the fence it is hard to mow. For such spots we choose shade-tolerant mixes, reinforce the slope with a geogrid or drop grass altogether in favour of ground-cover plants, gravel, bark or paving.

This decision is made at the design stage, not after the first summer when the lawn is already trampled or scorched. That way the plot looks well kept and maintenance does not turn into a weekly fight against bald patches.

How do you care for a lawn in its first year?

  • watering — frequent and moderate at the start, then less often but deeper; easiest to leave to automatic irrigation
  • mowing — not too short, so the grass does not scorch
  • feeding by season — different fertilisers in spring and autumn
  • keep off the fresh lawn until it has rooted
  • clear leaves in autumn so the lawn does not rot under them

It is worth edging the lawn by the terrace, paths and pool: the edge stays neat and the grass does not creep into the paving joints.

How much do a lawn and a garden design cost?

The choice of lawn, the irrigation layout and base preparation are part of the landscape design project — from $50 per 100 m². For a 1 000 m² plot the design costs from 10 × $50 = $500. Laying the lawn and preparing the base are costed in the estimate: the price depends on the area, soil condition and chosen option.

Questions and answers

Which is better: turf or seeded lawn?

Turf — when you need a result straight away or the plot has a slope. Seed — for large areas, shade and places where you can wait a season.

When can a lawn be sown?

Best in spring or in late summer and early autumn, when it is warm but not hot.

Does a lawn need automatic irrigation?

Not necessarily, but it makes care much easier, especially in the first year and on plots you do not visit every day.

Why does a lawn go yellow in patches?

Most often because of the base: building rubble, standing water or uneven soil. That is why preparation matters more than the choice of lawn.
