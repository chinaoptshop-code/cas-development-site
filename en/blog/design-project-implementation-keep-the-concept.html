Implementing a design project: how not to lose the concept on site

CAS Development · 25 October 2026

Implementation is the stage at which a beautiful visualisation either becomes a real interior or gradually drifts away from the drawings. Here is where the concept is most often lost and how to avoid it.

Why does the concept get lost on site?

A design project is created on paper and in 3D, but it is built by the hands of a crew. There is a gap between these two stages: what the designer sees as a line of light, a niche or the junction of two materials is just a line in a drawing for the tradesman — and sometimes not even that. If nobody watches the transition from design to walls, the interior gradually becomes “roughly like the picture”.

The good news: almost all losses of the concept are predictable. They repeat from project to project, so they can be closed off in advance.

Where is the concept most often lost?

  • the crew works without a full set of drawings, “from a photo” or from verbal explanations
  • a material is replaced with a similar one because the right one is out of stock or more expensive, and nobody checks how the substitute will look in the interior
  • sockets, switches and lights are installed “where it is convenient” rather than to the plan
  • complex details — concealed doors, niches, shadow-gap profiles, large-format tiles — are simplified
  • the size of built-in furniture is not checked against the real walls after rough works
  • the designer does not see the site after handing over the design, so deviations are noticed only once they are covered by finishes

Design implementation

What does precise implementation of a design project need?

For design project implementation to go without losses, three things are needed: full drawings, control of materials and a person who makes sure everything is done to the design.

  1. Working drawings: plans, wall elevations, lighting, socket and plumbing layouts, specifications of materials and furniture. The crew must work from them, not from an idea of “something similar”.
  2. Approval of substitutions: no material is replaced without the decision of the design's author. If a substitution is unavoidable, it is assessed in the context of the whole interior, not on its own.
  3. Designer supervision: the designer's visits at key stages, checks of details before they are closed, answers to the crew's questions. This is what separates an interior “to the design” from one that is “similar to the design”.

Why is designer supervision needed during implementation?

Designer supervision catches mistakes before they are covered with tiles or plasterboard, when they are still relatively easy to fix. Later it is much more expensive, and sometimes impossible without dismantling. So supervision works not as a formality but as a way to see deviations in time. It matters especially when a project has many precise details and built-in furniture.

Why give design and renovation to one team?

When one studio does the design and another does the renovation, responsibility becomes blurred. The crew thinks the drawings are “inaccurate”, the designer thinks the crew did not follow them, and the client ends up in the middle. This is often where disputes about substitutions, extra works and estimate changes arise.

At CAS Development, design and renovation are carried out by one team. The authors of the design and the builders work together, questions on site are solved without sending drawings between contractors, and substitutions are agreed with the people who came up with the interior. Another practical consequence: if CAS carries out the renovation to your design, the estimate stays within ±5%. How a turnkey renovation works is explained on a separate page.

Questions and answers

What is design project implementation?

It is turning drawings and visualisations into a real property: renovation or construction to the design, control of materials and compliance with the drawings, and designer supervision.

Can a design be implemented by another crew?

Yes, but then full drawings, approval of substitutions and the author's site visits become especially important. One team for design and renovation simplifies control and reduces the risk of deviations.

How much does a renovation to a design project cost at CAS?

A turnkey renovation starts from $550/m². The exact sum is set by the estimate, and if CAS carries out the renovation to the design, the estimate stays within ±5%.
