How to choose an interior designer: 10 questions before signing a contract

CAS Development · 21 October 2026

The choice of designer affects not only how your apartment looks but also the renovation budget. Here is how to choose an interior designer: 10 questions to ask before signing a contract, what to look for in a portfolio and which signals should put you on guard.

10 questions for a designer

  1. Do you have completed projects, not just visualisations? Ask for photos of finished apartments.
  2. What exactly does the project include: are there working drawings and specifications?
  3. How many layout options will you show?
  4. How many rounds of 3D revisions are included in the price?
  5. Who will carry out the renovation, and can you do it yourselves?
  6. Do you provide a renovation estimate, and how accurate is it?
  7. Is there designer supervision, and how much does it cost?
  8. What is the design timeline and what does it depend on?
  9. How are agreements recorded: contract, payment stages, certificates?
  10. Will you help with turnkey procurement — furniture and materials?

Not only the answers matter but also how they are given. A good designer explains specifically: shows sample drawings, says what is included in the price and what is paid separately, and does not promise what they cannot guarantee.

How to choose an interior designer by portfolio

Look for projects similar to yours in size and task. Beautiful renders are good, but photos of completed projects show whether the studio can take a design through to the result. Compare the 3D and photos of the same interior: if they are close, the project was well thought out and the renovation followed the drawings.

Ask to see sample working drawings and a specification from a real project. It is they, not the pictures, that determine how smoothly the renovation will go.

Also pay attention to the variety of work. If all the interiors in a portfolio look the same, the designer probably works in one style and will fit your apartment to it. If the projects differ, they know how to listen to the client.

What the contract should include

  • the list of documents you will receive: layout, 3D, drawings, specifications, estimate
  • the number of layout options and revision rounds
  • work stages and payment for each stage
  • the design timeline and what affects it
  • terms of designer supervision, if needed
  • who is responsible for the result if the same team does the renovation

The more detail on the scope of work, the fewer disputes later. If the contract simply says “design project”, ask for an annex listing the documents with an example of each.

A freelance designer or a studio

A freelance designer can be a good choice if you only need a concept and will organise the renovation yourself. But then the coordination falls on you: finding a crew, reconciling drawings with the tradesmen, controlling purchases and resolving discrepancies between the design and reality.

A full-cycle interior design studio takes these tasks on. The designer, estimator and crew work in one team, so issues on site are solved internally rather than between several contractors. For you this means one point of contact and one party responsible for the result.

Red flags

  • a price well below the market with no explanation of what is excluded
  • no contract or payment stages
  • “the crew will make the drawings themselves”
  • refusal to show completed projects
  • promises of an exact renovation sum before measuring

Another signal is when the designer asks no questions about your lifestyle, budget and plans for the apartment and immediately offers a ready-made style. A good project starts with a brief, not with pictures.

Common client mistakes

  • choosing a designer only by their style on social media without asking about the project scope
  • comparing prices per m² without comparing what they include
  • not discussing the renovation budget at the start — and ending up with a design that does not fit the money
  • hiring a designer and a crew separately without agreeing who is responsible for discrepancies

Checklist before the first meeting

  • the apartment plan, or at least its area and number of rooms
  • photos of the current condition of the rooms
  • examples of interiors you like and dislike
  • a list of zones and wishes from everyone who will live in the apartment
  • an approximate budget for the design, renovation and furniture

With this preparation the first consultation takes less time, and the designer can say right away what is realistic within your budget.

Do I need a designer for a small apartment?

Yes: in a small apartment every centimetre matters, and good planning and storage give the biggest effect.
