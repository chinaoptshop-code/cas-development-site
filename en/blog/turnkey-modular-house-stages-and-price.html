Turnkey modular house: how it is built and what makes up the price

CAS Development · 18 November 2026

A turnkey modular house is assembled from ready-made units: most of the work is done in the factory, and what remains on the plot is the foundation, installation and connections. We explain who this technology suits, how construction goes stage by stage, what the turnkey price includes and what is priced separately.

What is a modular house?

A modular house consists of volumetric units made in a factory: the frame, insulation of walls, floor and roof, windows, interior finishes, electrical and pipe runs. The finished modules are brought to the plot, set on the foundation and joined together. What is left to do on site is the roof and facade at the joints, the service connections and the finishing of the seams inside.

The main limitation of the technology is the size of a module that can be transported by road. That is why the layout is made up of several units, and large spans, a double-height space or wide panoramic windows have to be resolved in the design in advance.

Who is a modular house right for?

  • permanent living, provided the insulation and heating are designed for winter
  • a guest house on the plot next to the main one
  • a holiday resort or glamping site that needs several identical cabins
  • a country house where it matters that builders spend as little time on the plot as possible

If you are planning several guest cabins, start with a master plan of the whole site: where the cabins will stand, how services are brought in, where the parking and paths go. More on this is on the turnkey holiday resort page.

What are the stages from design to moving in?

  1. Site visit and topographic survey: the relief, access for the lorry and crane.
  2. Architectural design to suit the module dimensions and the layout of the house.
  3. Estimate with separate lines for the foundation, delivery, installation and connections.
  4. Foundation to suit the soil on the plot; while it gains strength, the modules are made in the factory.
  5. Delivery and installation of the modules.
  6. Joining the modules and services, roof and facade at the joints.
  7. Connection to external utilities, finishing the joints inside.
  8. System checks and handover with a signed certificate.

The fact that work in the factory and on the plot runs in parallel is the main advantage of modular construction. The weather affects quality less, and there are far fewer builders on your plot than with conventional construction.

The other side of this speed is that decisions have to be made earlier. The layout, window sizes, finishes, and the positions of sockets and plumbing are agreed before the modules go into production. Moving a wall or a pipe in an assembled module is difficult and expensive, so it is worth allowing enough time for the design stage.

What does the turnkey price include?

Different companies understand the word “turnkey” differently, so look at the list of works. In our estimate a turnkey modular house means:

  • modules with insulation, windows, an entrance door and finishes
  • a foundation to suit the soil on the plot
  • delivery, installation and joining of the modules
  • heating and water supply, sanitaryware, sockets and lighting
  • handover with a signed certificate

Priced separately, by estimate, are the connection to external utilities, a borehole and septic tank, furniture and appliances, terraces and landscaping of the plot.

How much does a turnkey modular house cost?

The guide price for a turnkey modular house is from $1,100/m². For a 60 m² guest house that is from 60 m² × $1,100 = $66,000, and for a 100 m² house from 100 m² × $1,100 = $110,000. The architectural design of a house costs from $12/m²: for 100 m² that is from 100 m² × $12 = $1,200. The exact amount comes from the estimate based on the design.

The price is affected most by:

  • the area and the number of modules
  • the delivery distance and access to the plot
  • the type of foundation for the soil
  • the heating system
  • the class of finishes

How the budget of a house breaks down overall is set out on the turnkey house price page.

Modular, timber frame or aerated concrete: which to choose?

A modular house makes sense when predictable time on site and a standard layout matter. A timber-frame house assembled on site gives more freedom in the layout and window sizes. Aerated concrete is chosen for its solid walls and a technology many people are used to. A detailed comparison is in the article timber frame, aerated concrete or timber beams.

Questions and answers

How much does a turnkey modular house cost?

From $1,100/m². For a 60 m² house — from $66,000, for 100 m² — from $110,000. The exact amount comes from the estimate based on the design.

Can you live in a modular house in winter?

Yes, if the walls, floor and roof are insulated as designed and the heating is sized for winter. We build these parameters into the design before the modules are made.

What is not included in the turnkey price?

Connection to external utilities, a borehole and septic tank, furniture and appliances, terraces and landscaping. These are priced separately, by estimate.

Can a modular house have a large living room?

Yes, but it has to be planned in the design in advance: the layout is assembled from several modules, and a large span is resolved at the joint between units.
