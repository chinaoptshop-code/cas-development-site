Accepting an apartment renovation: a checklist before you sign the certificate

CAS Development · 18 November 2026

A signed certificate means you have accepted the work, and defects that were visible during the inspection are harder to prove later. We have put together a checklist for accepting an apartment renovation: what to prepare, what to check in each room, which documents to get from the contractor and how to record your remarks.

Why accept a renovation stage by stage, not only at the end?

The most important parts of a renovation are hidden under the finishes: electrics, pipes, waterproofing, screed. After the tiles and paint you can no longer see them, so concealed works are accepted before they are covered, with photos and a certificate. We described how to organise this in the article on technical supervision of a renovation.

The final acceptance is a check of what can be seen and what can be switched on. It is best done in daylight, in an apartment without furniture and after cleaning, with time to spare. Rushing the acceptance is the most common reason small defects stay for good.

Invite the person who is responsible for the site on the contractor's side: the site foreman or the manager. If the renovation followed a design project, it is useful to invite the designer too. They will notice faster where the work departs from the drawings: a different socket height, a light fitting that has shifted, the wrong shade of paint.

What should you bring to the handover?

  • the design project or drawings, the estimate and the contract
  • earlier certificates and photos of concealed works
  • a long straightedge or spirit level, a tape measure
  • a torch: raking light shows unevenness in walls and ceilings
  • a phone charger or night light to test the sockets
  • masking tape or stickers to mark defects

Designer and technical supervision

How do you check walls, ceilings and floors?

  • hold the straightedge against walls and corners in several places: there should be no waves or leaning
  • shine the torch along the wall and ceiling: this shows runs, trowel marks and uneven joints
  • check the paint: an even tone, no stains or thin patches, especially around switches and behind doors
  • wallpaper: the seams do not open up and the pattern matches
  • floor: it does not creak or flex, the skirting sits tight, thresholds between rooms are level
  • the dimensions of niches and openings match the drawings, because furniture has already been ordered for them

What to check in the bathroom and tiling?

  • tiles: straight joints, an even grout width, neat cuts, no chips
  • tap the tiles: a hollow sound may mean a void underneath
  • pour water in the shower area: it should run to the drain and not stand in puddles
  • open all the taps and flush: no leaks under the basin, bath or toilet
  • the sealant is even, with no gaps in corners or around sanitaryware
  • access hatches to inspection points and meters open

How do you check electrics, lighting and services?

  • test every socket, including those behind appliances and in the kitchen splashback
  • switches turn on exactly the lighting group shown in the design, and the scenes work
  • the breakers in the distribution board are labelled, and the “Test” button on the protective devices trips
  • underfloor heating, air conditioners, ventilation and the heat recovery unit switch on and work
  • radiators heat up and their valves open
  • internet, intercom and video surveillance are connected, if they were in the scope of works

What to look for in doors, windows and built-ins?

  • doors close without effort and do not catch the floor; locks and handles work
  • windows open in all modes, reveals are even, sills are free of scratches
  • kitchen and wardrobe fronts are aligned, drawers slide smoothly, appliances are built in as drawn

Which documents should you get after the renovation?

  • a certificate of completed works with the list of works and the amount
  • diagrams or photos of concealed services: where the cables and pipes run, so that you do not drill into them later
  • manuals and warranty cards for the boiler, water heater, air conditioners and appliances
  • the contractor's warranty obligations: the term and what to do if a defect shows up later
  • leftover materials: tiles, paint with the colour code, grout — in case of minor repairs

How do you record remarks at the handover?

Write everything you find in a single list: the room, what is wrong, a photo, the deadline for fixing it. Both parties sign the list, and the certificate is signed after a repeat inspection, once the defects have been remedied. If the remarks are minor, they can be written into the certificate with a deadline. The main thing is not to sign a “clean” certificate while the list is still open.

How acceptance works at CAS

In our renovations, technical and designer supervision are built into the process: we accept concealed works before they are covered, and every 1–2 weeks you receive a photo report and sign stage-by-stage certificates. We come to the final acceptance together with you, with the design and a list of checkpoints. The warranty on works is 12 months. If another contractor is doing the renovation, technical supervision can be ordered separately — the terms and cost are agreed individually; more details are on the designer and technical supervision page.

Questions and answers

What should I check when accepting an apartment renovation?

The flatness of walls and ceilings, tiles and joints, water drainage in the shower, leaks under sanitaryware, every socket and lighting group, how doors, windows and built-in furniture work, as well as the documents and diagrams of concealed services.

Can I sign the certificate with remarks?

Yes, if the remarks are minor: they are written into the certificate with a deadline for fixing them. If there are many defects, it is better to wait for the corrections first and carry out a repeat inspection.

How can I check works that are already covered by finishes?

You cannot, without opening them up. That is why concealed works are accepted before they are covered, with photos and a certificate, and the service diagrams are kept after the renovation.

Can I order technical supervision if another crew is doing the renovation?

Yes. We take on technical supervision of another contractor's site separately; the terms and cost are agreed individually.
