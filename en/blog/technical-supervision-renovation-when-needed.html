Technical supervision of an apartment renovation: when you need it and what it checks

CAS Development · 1 November 2026

Technical supervision of a renovation is needed when you cannot be on site every day or are not sure you can judge the quality of concealed works. Its job is to spot a mistake while it is still cheap to fix. Here is what technical supervision checks, when it makes sense and how to organise it.

What is technical supervision of a renovation?

Technical supervision is an independent check of how construction works are carried out: whether they match the design, the material manufacturers' technology and the contract. The supervisor does not build or manage the crew. They look at the site through the client's eyes, but with professional knowledge: what exactly should be done, how to check it and what a deviation leads to.

The most valuable part of the job is concealed works. Electrics, plumbing, waterproofing, screed, reinforcement and insulation become invisible once closed. If a defect is missed, it is found only when it shows itself — as a leak, a crack or a tripping breaker. Fixing it then means tearing out finished surfaces.

What technical supervision does not do

  • it does not manage the crew or plan daily work — that is the site manager's job
  • it does not buy materials or handle deliveries
  • it does not change the design — deviations are agreed with the designer and the client
  • it does not replace the contractor's warranty: responsibility for the works stays with the contractor

Knowing these limits matters so you do not expect the impossible. The supervisor is your eyes and expertise on site, not another party taking over the contractor's responsibility. A good supervisor does not argue with the crew but records facts and proposes how to fix them.

How does it differ from designer supervision?

The two services are often confused, but the difference is in what they control. Designer supervision is the designer's control: does the result match the concept, materials and details. Technical supervision is the engineer's control: are the works done correctly from a technical point of view.

  • designer supervision checks that a light fitting is where it was planned and that the tile joint lines up with the niche
  • technical supervision checks that the wiring to the fitting is installed correctly and that there is waterproofing under the tiles
  • designer supervision answers the crew's questions about drawings and details
  • technical supervision records deviations from the technology and requires them to be fixed before the next stage

A complex project needs both. More on the designer's role on site in our article on designer supervision.

Design implementation

When is technical supervision especially needed?

  • the renovation is done by a contractor you have not worked with before
  • you cannot visit the site regularly — you live in another city or abroad
  • the design has complex services: underfloor heating, ventilation, air conditioning, smart home
  • you are moving wet areas or building a bathroom in a new place
  • the budget is large and a mistake in rough works will be expensive
  • several independent contractors work on site and nobody is responsible for the whole result

If one general contractor runs the renovation to a design with its own internal quality control, the need for external supervision is smaller — but even then it helps to know which works to check yourself or from photo reports. For example, agree that before walls and floors are closed you receive detailed photos with a tape measure and can come and look, and that pipe pressure tests are recorded in a separate report.

What does it check in concealed works?

Technical supervision checks concealed works first of all — the ones most important to see before they are closed; the list is below. You can use the list even without a supervisor, comparing it with the contractor's photo reports.

Electrics

  • cable routes match the design, positions are photographed with a tape measure
  • cable cross-sections match the load, with separate lines for high-power appliances
  • the distribution board is neat, circuits are labelled, protective devices are fitted
  • back boxes are set at design height and level

Plumbing and wet areas

  • water and drainage pipework is pressure-tested before closing
  • drainage has proper falls, access points are left where they can be reached
  • waterproofing runs up the walls, joints and corners are taped
  • concealed frames are rigidly fixed, outlets are where the drawings show

Substrates and finishes

  • the screed is level, without cracks or voids, and cured before covering
  • walls and corners are true, checked with a straightedge and level
  • tiles have no voids beneath, joints are even, the layout follows the drawing
  • concealed doors and shadow gaps are flush with the wall

Ventilation, air conditioning and underfloor heating

  • duct and refrigerant routes follow the design and stay accessible for maintenance
  • AC condensate drains have a fall and discharge where planned
  • heating loops are laid at the right spacing and tested before the screed
  • sensors and thermostats are placed where they are convenient to use

Does technical supervision pay off?

Yes, supervision pays off, even though it costs money. The saving does not come from a lower renovation price — it comes from fewer reworks, and rework always costs more than doing it right the first time.

  • a waterproofing defect found before tiling is a local fix; after, the bathroom is torn out
  • a wrong cable route spotted before plastering is a small fix; after, it means chasing a finished wall
  • an uneven screed found before parquet is levelled; after, the flooring is replaced
  • a loose concealed frame found before cladding is tightened; after, the boxing is dismantled

Supervision also gives you a documented basis for talking to the contractor: sign-off reports for concealed works, photos and a list of remarks. This matters especially when payments are tied to stages — you pay for a stage whose quality is confirmed. And a crew that knows concealed works will be checked simply works more carefully.

How do you organise supervision and when should it visit?

  1. Agree the list of key checks before works start, following the stage schedule.
  2. Write into the contract that concealed works are not closed without inspection.
  3. Choose the format: site visits, photo reports with measurements, sign-off reports.
  4. Keep a list of remarks with deadlines — and check the fixes.
  5. Tie the next payments to closing the remarks from the previous stage.

Visits follow the schedule, not the calendar. The key moments are before electrics and plumbing are closed, before the screed, after waterproofing wet areas, before tiling and flooring, before installing bespoke furniture and at final handover. Visits are easy to combine with regular photo reports and online site control. Contract terms and payment procedures are described on our contract, warranty and payment page.

How does it differ in a new build and older housing?

In a new build supervision often starts before the renovation — with what the developer handed over: the geometry of walls and ceilings, the screed, windows, ventilation shafts and services. Defects found are best recorded and resolved with the developer before your own works start. In older housing the focus is different: the condition of slabs and walls after demolition, old risers, hidden leaks, ventilation. Here supervision helps decide on additional works in time and put them into the estimate before they become a surprise.

Supervision is done by civil engineers with experience on renovation sites. Ask for sample reports from other projects, agree how remarks are recorded and how quickly the specialist can come for an unscheduled check. The supervisor should be independent of the contractor being checked and report directly to you.

Which signals say you need supervision?

  • the contractor closes walls before you have seen the wiring
  • the answer to “where is the waterproofing?” is “it is there, don't worry”
  • photo reports are irregular or lack detail
  • works deviate from the drawings “because it is easier”
  • changes in scope keep appearing without explanation

Each of these signals is a reason to pause and ask for an independent check while fixes do not yet require demolition.

Questions and answers

What is technical supervision of a renovation?

An independent check that the works match the design, the material technology and the contract. Most important is checking concealed works before they are closed.

How does it differ from designer supervision?

Designer supervision checks that the result matches the designer's concept; technical supervision checks that the works are done correctly from an engineering point of view. Complex projects benefit from both.

How much does technical supervision cost at CAS?

If another contractor does the renovation, we set the terms and cost individually, depending on the property, scope and visit schedule.

Do I need supervision if one company does a turnkey renovation?

Less so, if the company has its own quality control and records concealed works. But asking for photos of concealed works before closing is worthwhile in any case.

Which works are most important to check?

Electrics, plumbing, waterproofing of wet areas, screed and wall geometry — everything that becomes invisible after finishing.
