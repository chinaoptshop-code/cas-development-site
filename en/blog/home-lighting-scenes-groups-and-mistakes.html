Apartment lighting: scenes, lighting groups and common mistakes

CAS Development · 16 November 2026

A single chandelier in the middle of the ceiling rarely makes an apartment cosy. Light, like furniture, is planned in advance: where you read, cook and watch a film, and what should glow at night. We explain how apartment lighting works in a design project, what scenes and groups are, and which mistakes are easy to avoid at the drawings stage.

Which three layers of light does every room need?

Designers split apartment lighting into three layers. Each has its own job, and together they give light that can be adjusted to any situation.

  • ambient — even, diffused light to see the whole room: ceiling fittings, track systems, concealed perimeter lighting
  • task — where precision is needed: above the kitchen worktop, by the mirror, over the desk, next to the reading chair
  • accent — for mood and depth: lighting niches, pictures, shelves, a textured wall, floor and table lamps

The mistake we see most often is ambient light only. The room is evenly lit but flat and the same by day and by night. Add task and accent light, and the same space looks different.

What are lighting scenes and why do you need them?

A scene is a set of fittings that switch on together for a particular situation. A living room usually has several: bright light for cleaning or guests, soft evening light — concealed lighting and a floor lamp — and cinema, with only faint lighting behind the TV. In the kitchen, task light above the worktop and separate light over the dining table.

For each scene the design defines groups — which fittings are connected to one switch or one dimmer channel. Groups are built into the wiring before the renovation: afterwards it is hard to add a separate cable to the fitting you need.

What lighting does each room need?

Living room — the most scenes: ambient light, lighting for niches and shelves, a floor lamp by the sofa, light over the coffee or dining table. If the TV is on the wall, soft lighting behind it reduces eye strain.

Kitchen — task light along the whole worktop, with no shadows from the wall cabinets: linear lighting under them or spotlights above the worktop edge. Over the island or dining table — pendants hung at a height that does not dazzle people sitting down.

Bedroom — a minimum of bright overhead light, more bedside lamps and soft concealed lighting. Switches by the bed so you do not have to get up. Night light in the hallway and bathroom that does not wake you: low-level lighting with a motion sensor.

Bathroom — even light at the mirror from both sides or above it, so the face is lit without shadows, and moisture-protected fittings in the shower zone.

Which colour temperature and lamps should you choose?

Colour temperature determines whether light is warm, like an incandescent bulb, or cool, like daylight. Living rooms usually get warm light, work zones neutral light. The main rule is one temperature within one space: lamps of different shades in the same room look untidy.

  • one colour temperature for all lamps in a room
  • dimmers for the living room, bedroom and dining area
  • quality drivers for LED strips so the light does not flicker
  • access to the power supplies of concealed lighting — a hatch or removable panel

Where should switches and sockets go?

Switches are placed along the routes people take: ambient light by the room entrance, scenes by the sofa and bed, two-way switches at both ends of the hallway. Sockets for floor and table lamps are planned together with the furniture layout so you do not need extension leads.

If you want to control light from your phone or by voice, the smart home system is built in at the wiring stage: separate lines, a place for the controller, a cable to each group. We explained how services are planned in a turnkey apartment in the article on building services.

Which apartment lighting mistakes should you avoid?

  • one chandelier per room with no other light
  • spotlights in a regular grid across the whole ceiling — the light is flat and the ceiling looks full of holes
  • no light above the kitchen worktop
  • pendants over the table too high or too low
  • switches behind the door or far from the bed
  • LED strip without a profile and diffuser — individual dots are visible
  • concealed lighting installed but no access left to the power supply

Questions and answers

How many lighting scenes does a living room need?

Usually two or three: bright ambient light, soft evening light and cinema. For each scene, the design defines groups of fittings and switches.

What colour temperature should I choose for an apartment?

Living rooms usually get warm light, work zones neutral light. The key is one temperature within one space.

When should lighting be planned?

Before the renovation, in the design project: lighting groups and outlets for fittings are built into the wiring before plastering and ceilings.

Is a lighting plan included in the design project?

Yes. A design project from $30/m² includes a fitting plan, groups, switches and a specification.
