Renovating a new-build apartment: 8 mistakes that are expensive to fix

CAS Development · 23 November 2026

A new apartment looks like an easy job: the walls are straight, the pipes are fresh and there is nothing to demolish. But the mistakes are built in during the first weeks and only show once everything is covered with tiles and paint. We go through eight mistakes that force a new-build apartment renovation in Kyiv to be redone, and explain how to avoid each one.

Which mistakes are made before the renovation even starts?

Before the start, two mistakes cost the most: starting without a design and not inspecting the apartment after the developer. How the renovation itself runs is described on the new-build apartment renovation page.

1. Starting without a design to move in sooner

Without drawings, decisions are made on site: where the bed goes, how many sockets the kitchen needs, which way the doors open. Each decision looks minor, but together they add up to rework, idle crews and an estimate that grows every week. You need a design before the start, at least for the layout, electrics, plumbing and bathroom wall elevations.

2. Trusting the developer’s walls and screed without checking

A developer’s “white box” finish saves money only when the base is sound. Before the start, check the geometry of walls and corners, the screed (cracks, voids, level differences), the electrical wiring and the position of the risers. If the plaster is coming away and the sockets are not where the furniture will stand, it is cheaper to redo it now than to bend the whole interior around it. We worked out how the condition of the apartment affects the budget in the article on new-build apartment renovation cost.

Which building-services mistakes are the most expensive to fix?

In building services, the costliest things to fix are wiring and plumbing laid out without a furniture plan, and savings on waterproofing and concealed works: later, all of it is hidden under the finishes.

3. Running electrics and water without a furniture and appliance plan

A socket behind a wardrobe, a water outlet that misses the sink, a switch hidden behind an open door: all of these come from wiring “to the standard”. First you need a furniture plan and a list of appliances with dimensions, and only then are sockets, outlets and lighting circuits placed to suit them. The same goes for air conditioning: the pipe runs are laid before plastering, even if you buy the units themselves later.

4. Saving on waterproofing and concealed works

Bathroom waterproofing, pipe joints, cable cross-sections and the breakers in the distribution board cost little against the whole renovation. Yet they decide whether the tiles will have to come off after you have moved in. Concealed works are accepted before they are covered: with photos, or better still with a signed certificate. What goes wrong with timing and the sequence of works?

Timing goes wrong in two ways: settlement and curing pauses are ignored, and furniture and doors are ordered only after the renovation.

5. Ignoring building settlement and curing pauses

A new building settles in its first years, and hairline cracks can appear where walls, partitions and the ceiling meet. This is designed into the details: reinforced plaster, expansion joints in the screed, flexible junctions. The other half of the same mistake is haste. Screed and plaster have to dry before the final finishes, and there is no way to speed that up without harming the floor and the paint. What the timeline depends on is covered in the article on how long a new-build apartment renovation takes.

6. Ordering the kitchen, wardrobes and doors after the renovation

Made-to-order furniture and doors take more than a week to produce, and delivery of furniture and lighting directly from factories in China takes 35–70 days. If you order them after the finishing works, the completed apartment will stand empty. Measurements for the kitchen and wardrobes are taken after plastering and screeding, and the order is placed straight away so that installation coincides with the end of the renovation.

What goes wrong with the estimate, the contract and the building rules?

There are two mistakes here: accepting an estimate without quantities and not finding out the building’s renovation rules.

7. Agreeing to an estimate without quantities

A one-line estimate that says “turnkey renovation” does not let you compare offers and does not protect you from extra charges. In a working estimate, works and materials are itemised with quantities, while finishing materials, furniture and appliances are listed separately. Set aside a separate budget for furniture, lighting and appliances: people often remember them when the renovation money has already been allocated. We explained how to check the document in the article on how to read a renovation estimate.

8. Not finding out the renovation rules of the building

In a new building, dozens of apartments are renovated at the same time, and the management company usually sets its own rules. Before the start, find out:

  • the hours when noisy works are allowed
  • how to book the goods lift and whether the cabin and lobby must be protected
  • where and when construction waste is removed
  • how to arrange passes for the crew and deliveries
  • whether changes to the facade need approval: air conditioners, balcony glazing

If you skip this, the works may be stopped at the least convenient moment.

What should you check before the renovation starts?

Before a new-build renovation starts, check six points:

  1. the design: layout, electrics, plumbing, bathroom wall elevations
  2. an inspection of the apartment: walls, screed, windows, distribution board, risers
  3. a furniture plan and a list of appliances with dimensions
  4. an estimate with quantities and a separate section for finishing materials
  5. a contract with a stage schedule and payment against certificates
  6. the management company’s rules and the contact of the person in charge

Questions and answers

Which mistakes in a new-build renovation are the most expensive?

Starting without a design, running electrics and water without a furniture plan, and saving on waterproofing and concealed works. Fixing them means removing finishes, which is why they cost the most.

Do I have to wait for the building to settle before renovating?

There is no need to wait for years. Settlement is handled in the detailing: the plaster is reinforced, and expansion joints in the screed and flexible junctions are provided.

Can I keep the developer’s plaster and screed?

Yes, if the base is sound and matches the design. This is decided after an inspection: the geometry of the walls, the screed, the wiring and the position of the risers are checked.
