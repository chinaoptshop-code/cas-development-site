An office in a business centre: what to agree with the landlord before the fit-out

CAS Development · 26 October 2026

An office fit-out in a business centre starts not with demolition but with a conversation with the landlord. We explain what to agree in advance so the work does not stall halfway and the estimate does not grow because of the building's requirements.

Why are approvals the first stage of a fit-out in a business centre?

In leased premises you do not control everything. The building has its own rules: how the services work, when noise is allowed, how materials are delivered, who is responsible for fire safety. If you find out about them during the works, the schedule slips and some of the work has to be redone.

That is why, even before the estimate, we gather the business centre's requirements and build them into the design, schedule and budget. It takes a little time at the start but saves weeks during the works.

What should you check in the lease?

  • what you may change in the premises, and what only with written approval
  • who pays for changes to the building's services
  • in what condition the premises must be returned at the end of the lease
  • whether the building has its own contractors for certain systems
  • what documents are needed for the crew to get access

Legal aspects of the lease are best checked with your lawyer. Our job is to translate the agreements with the landlord into technical solutions and an estimate.

Do the design and layout changes need approval?

Most business centres ask to see the design before work starts: partition layout, location of toilets and kitchen, load on the electrical supply. It helps when the office design and working drawings are ready for this conversation — approval goes faster, and questions are resolved on paper rather than on site.

What must be agreed on ventilation, air conditioning and electrics?

A business centre's services are often shared across the whole floor. So it is important to find out in advance:

  • how much power is allocated to your office and whether it can be increased
  • how to connect ventilation and air conditioning to the building's systems
  • where the services run and how to route low-voltage cabling
  • how to relocate fire alarm detectors and sprinklers to suit the new layout
  • whether a kitchen and toilets can go where you want them

When can works be done, and how do materials get in?

Neighbours on the floor are working, so most buildings have rules on noisy works. We agree the hours for noisy work with you and the building management, and fix the work schedule and handover date in the contract. Separately we arrange the goods lift, delivery times for materials, waste removal and a storage area.

If your team already works in this office, the site can be split into phases: one zone first, then another. More on this approach in the article office renovation without stopping the business.

What should the estimate for a business-centre fit-out include?

The building's requirements mean not only approvals but money and time. That is why the estimate for an office fit-out in a business centre separately includes connection to the building services, relocating detectors and sprinklers for the new layout, protecting common areas during the works and waste removal according to building rules. If the building only lets its own contractors touch certain systems, that must also be visible in the estimate and schedule.

Such an estimate is more honest: you see the full picture at once rather than learning about extra costs halfway through. If CAS carries out the renovation to our design, the final total stays within ±5% of the agreed estimate.

What should you check before works start?

  1. get the rules for carrying out works from the landlord
  2. agree the layout and services design
  3. find out the available power and the connection points for building services
  4. agree the hours for noisy works and the procedure for material deliveries
  5. arrange access for the crew
  6. build all requirements into the estimate and the schedule in the contract

Questions and answers

Does an office fit-out need to be agreed with the landlord?

As a rule, yes: layout changes, changes to the services and the work schedule are agreed with the business centre. The exact terms depend on the lease.

Can the work be done while neighbours on the floor are working?

Yes. We agree the hours for noisy work with you and the building management, and fix the work schedule in the contract.

Who negotiates with the business centre's technical team?

We take on the technical issues — design, services, crew access — together with your representative.
