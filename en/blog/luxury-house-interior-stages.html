Luxury house interior: the stages from design to furniture

CAS Development · 25 October 2026

A luxury house interior is built in stages, and each stage shapes the next: from the brief and concept to working drawings, finishing and furniture installation. Here is the order that works, what to approve at every step and how to build a budget without surprises.

How a house interior differs from an apartment

In an apartment the designer works within a finished shell: walls, windows and risers are already in place. A private house offers more freedom, but also many more decisions: several floors, a staircase, double-height spaces, terraces and garden access, a boiler room, walk-in wardrobes, guest rooms, sometimes a pool or sauna. The interior here is not just finishes — it is the logic of how the family moves through the house.

The luxury level adds another layer: expensive materials, bespoke furniture, complex lighting and building services that do not forgive mistakes. So the key rule is simple — decisions on paper first, works on site second.

  • several floors, with the staircase as part of the interior
  • connection to the terrace, garden and landscape
  • service rooms: boiler room, laundry, storage
  • private and guest zones that do not overlap
  • complex services: underfloor heating, ventilation, smart home

Stage 1. Brief and concept

It all starts with a conversation about how the family lives. Who gets up first, where you have breakfast, how many guests come at weekends, whether you need a study, a gym, a hobby room, space for a wine or book collection. We record these scenarios in the brief and then turn them into zoning: what goes where and how the spaces connect.

The concept defines the style, palette, key materials and mood of each zone. At this stage we agree mood boards and references, then 3D visualisations of the main rooms: living room, kitchen-diner, master bedroom, bathroom. Visualisations are a tool to agree on the result before money is spent on materials.

It is worth naming a budget range already at the concept stage. It is not a limit for the designer but a guide: where natural stone makes sense and where quality porcelain will do; which furniture to make bespoke and which to buy off the shelf; where to put the accent and where to keep a calm background. Without a range, the concept risks becoming a beautiful picture that has to be redone after the estimate.

Stage 2. Interior and architecture together

The best time to start the interior is when the house is still on paper or at shell stage. You can then move a window to suit the bed or desk, build in niches for wardrobes and lighting, prepare a place for a fireplace and set ceiling heights for concealed curtain tracks and ventilation. Once walls are plastered, every such change means demolition.

If you are only planning to build, it makes sense to have one team handle both architecture and interior. More on building from scratch and turnkey — on our house construction page. Construction with CAS starts from $600 per m², turnkey from $1,100 per m².

Stage 3. Working drawings

Working drawings are what builders and furniture makers work from. For a luxury house they usually run to dozens of sheets: demolition and construction plans, electrical and sockets, lighting scenarios, plumbing, underfloor heating, ceilings, floors, wall elevations, junction details, stairs, built-in furniture, doors, plus specifications for materials and furniture with product codes.

The more detailed the drawings, the fewer questions on site and the more accurate the estimate. This is where the details that define a luxury interior are set: a shadow gap instead of a skirting board, flush doors, stair lighting, sockets hidden in joinery.

Stairs, double-height spaces and transitions

A house has elements an apartment does not, and they shape the impression of the interior most. The staircase is visible from several rooms, so it is designed like a sculpture: tread material, balustrade, lighting, the space under the flight. A double-height space adds volume and air but needs a well-chosen chandelier, maintenance access and a solution for curtains on tall windows.

Transitions matter just as much: how the living room floor continues onto the terrace, how the kitchen links to the dining area and garden, where the line between guest and private areas runs. Well-designed transitions make a house feel whole rather than a set of separate rooms.

Stage 4. Estimate and schedule

The estimate is built from the working drawings: works, materials, furniture, lighting and sanitary ware as separate line items. In parallel we draw up a schedule: when rough works happen, when services go in, when finishing starts and when deliveries arrive. For a house the delivery schedule is especially important, because there are many items and some are made to order.

A benchmark at our rates for a 300 m² house with a finished shell:

  • interior design: from $30 × 300 m² = from $9,000
  • turnkey renovation and finishing: from $550 × 300 m² = from $165,000
  • furnishing and procurement: from $250 × 300 m² = from $75,000

In total — from $249,000. This is the lower bound: stone, bespoke furniture, complex lighting and smart-home systems increase it. The exact figure comes from the estimate for your project, and if CAS carries out the renovation, we keep the final cost within ±5% of the estimate.

Stage 5. Finishing and quality control

During the works the main thing is that the design does not get simplified along the way. That takes control: the designer or project manager regularly checks that works match the drawings, that the right materials were delivered and that concealed details are installed correctly. Photo and video reports let you follow progress even if you do not live nearby or are abroad.

If your design was done by another designer, we can take it on — more on our design implementation page. Before starting we check that the drawing set is complete and add to it if needed.

Stage 6. Furniture, lighting, textiles and decor

Furnishing is the final layer that makes a house a home. Furniture, light fittings, sanitary ware, doors, textiles, rugs and decor are ordered from the project specification so that sizes, colours and materials match the drawings. Long-lead items are ordered while rough works are still under way.

  • bespoke and off-the-shelf furniture — on one specification
  • lighting: technical, decorative, control scenarios
  • textiles: curtains, rugs, throws, cushions
  • decor and art — last, once the space is assembled

Some items are easiest to order from our catalogue, Ozdoba (in Ukrainian): delivery from China takes 30–60 days and the minimum order starts at $500. After delivery we handle assembly, placement and final styling: furniture goes in according to plan, curtains and lights are hung, lighting scenes are set up and the house is handed over ready to live in.

What most often breaks the timeline and budget

  • layout changes after finishing works have started
  • choosing furniture and lighting later, when the walls are already done
  • materials without approved samples — and substitutions on site
  • several contractors without one person in charge

Each of these is solved by sequence: design and estimate first, then works, with procurement running in parallel with the renovation on one schedule.

Questions and answers

When should I start the interior design of a house?

Ideally at the house design or shell stage. Windows, niches, ceilings and services can then be prepared for the interior without rework.

How much does interior design for a house cost?

From $30 per m², with a minimum project fee of $2,500. For a 300 m² house that is from $9,000. We confirm the exact fee after the brief.

Can I order only the implementation of an existing design?

Yes. We check the drawings, prepare an estimate and run the renovation and procurement from your design.

How long do furniture and lighting take to arrive?

Delivery from our Ozdoba catalogue takes 30–60 days, so we place orders in parallel with the renovation to have everything on site by the end of works.
