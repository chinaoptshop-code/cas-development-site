Drainage and irrigation for your plot: what to lay before the lawn

CAS Development · 18 November 2026

Water on a plot is either too much or too little — and often both, in different months. We explain when you need drainage, how automatic irrigation is set up and why both systems are laid before the lawn and paths.

How can you tell your plot needs drainage?

The simplest way is to look at the plot after heavy rain or the spring thaw. If water stands in puddles, the lawn in low spots gets waterlogged and the basement or plinth turns damp, the soil cannot drain the water on its own.

  • puddles that take a long time to go
  • moss and soggy lawn in low spots
  • clay soil that lets water through poorly
  • a plot lower than the road or the neighbours
  • water by the foundation, damp plinth walls
  • a high water table — visible during ground investigation and earthworks

We do not judge by eye: we look at the terrain from the topographic survey, the soil type and where the water can leave the plot. Drainage without an outfall is just a pipe full of standing water.

What types of drainage systems are there?

A private plot usually combines several solutions. Which ones depends on where the water comes from: the sky, the roof or the ground.

  • surface drainage — channel drains with grates along paths, terraces, by the gates and wherever rain runs
  • storm drainage — water from the gutters goes into pipes rather than onto the apron
  • subsurface drainage — perforated pipes in geotextile and gravel, laid to a fall towards a chamber
  • foundation drainage along the footings if water reaches the house
  • inspection chambers so the system can be flushed

Where the collected water goes matters: into the storm sewer, a holding tank or a ditch. This is decided at the design stage, together with the grading plan.

What does an automatic irrigation system consist of?

Automatic irrigation is more than a hose with a timer. The system is split into zones, and each is watered the way it needs: a lawn needs one amount of water, shrubs and trees another, flowerbeds a third.

  • a water source and a pump if the mains pressure is not enough
  • a filter and a main line from the source to the plot
  • valves that switch zones on in turn
  • pop-up sprinklers for the lawn
  • drip lines for shrubs, trees and flowerbeds
  • a controller with a schedule and a rain sensor

The irrigation layout is laid over the planting plan: where the lawn is, where the beds are, where the house casts shade. That is why irrigation is designed together with the landscape design rather than bought after planting.

Why do drainage and irrigation go in before the lawn?

Both systems sit underground, under the lawn and partly under the paths. If you lay the paving and lawn first and then decide on irrigation, you will have to cut turf, lift slabs and level the soil again. So the order of works is always the same:

  1. Grading and falls.
  2. Drainage and storm sewer.
  3. Irrigation pipes and lighting cables, sleeves under the paths.
  4. Paving, edging and terraces.
  5. Topsoil, planting and lawn.
  6. Setting sprinklers flush with the lawn and adjusting the zones.

You can see how these networks are laid out in the “Landscape from scratch” animation on the landscape design page — X-ray mode shows the drainage, irrigation and cables under the lawn.

How do you maintain drainage and irrigation?

Drainage and irrigation need little attention, but regularly. Drainage chambers are inspected and flushed when needed. Irrigation is started up and checked zone by zone in spring, and in autumn, before the frosts, the pipes are drained or blown out so they do not burst. When we hand over the plot, we pass on a services plan so you do not have to hunt for pipes a few years later.

It is also worth thinking about the water for irrigation. If the plot has a borehole or a rainwater tank, irrigation can run from it rather than from the mains. This is decided at the design stage, because the source determines the pump, the filters and the space for the plant room.

Questions and answers

Does a flat plot need drainage?

It depends on the soil and the water table. If puddles stand after rain and the soil is clay, drainage is needed even on a flat plot.

When should irrigation go in — before or after the lawn?

Before. Pipes and cables are laid before paving and lawn, and the sprinklers are set to lawn level at the end.

What happens to irrigation in winter?

In autumn the system is winterised: the pipes are drained or blown out, and in spring it is started up and checked zone by zone.
