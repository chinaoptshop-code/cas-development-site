Restaurant building services: ventilation, grease traps and kitchen electrics before renovation

CAS Development · 4 November 2026

Guests see the dining room, the bar and the furniture, but a restaurant runs on what is in the ceiling and under the floor. Here is which networks to plan before the renovation so the kitchen works and the dining room does not smell of frying. 1. Where do restaurant building services start?

A restaurant's services cannot be designed without the kitchen technology: what is cooked, on what equipment, how many seats and what the peak-hour load is. That is why measuring and a 3D scan of the premises come first, then the kitchen layout with a kitchen technologist, and only then the networks and the dining room interior.

The most expensive mistake is to design the dining room first and “fit in” the kitchen later. Then the extract ducts run through the dining room and the sinks stand where there is no fall for drainage.

2. How do you separate kitchen and dining-room ventilation?

  • extraction over the hot kitchen — a separate system with its own fan and discharge
  • supply air into the kitchen — so the extraction does not “pull” air from the dining room
  • ventilation and air conditioning of the dining room — for the number of guests and the glazing
  • the duct route — agreed with the landlord or building management before work starts

The right balance is when the pressure in the dining room is slightly higher than in the kitchen: smells do not reach the guests. We put the duct routes into the ceiling design from the start, otherwise the ceiling will have to be lowered after installation.

3. Why does a restaurant need grease traps?

Kitchen waste water needs grease traps — under the sinks or one common trap at the outlet. It is important to plan falls, rodding points for cleaning and access to them, as well as separate floor drains in washing areas. Under the floor these networks run before the screed, so they can only be changed later by breaking it up.

4. How much power does the kitchen need?

  • the power capacity of the premises — check before signing the lease
  • separate circuits for hobs, ovens, cold rooms and dishwashers
  • a distribution board with spare capacity for equipment added within a year
  • emergency lighting and illuminated escape routes

If the allocated capacity is not enough, increasing it can take months. That is why we raise this question first, at the stage of choosing the premises.

Which low-voltage and safety systems are needed?

Besides the three main networks, a restaurant has several systems that should be built in before finishing. Tills and terminals, guest Wi-Fi, CCTV, music in the dining room, staff call — all of these are cables that must be in the walls and ceiling before skimming. Hot water for the kitchen needs a separate water heater or a connection with enough capacity, and washing areas need filters and water softening for dishwashers and the coffee machine.

Fire safety requirements for venues are stricter than for homes: alarms, emergency exits, fire-resistant materials in the kitchen. We take them into account in the design from the start so that the premises pass inspections before opening without rework.

How do services affect the dining room design?

Ceiling height, the position of the bar, places for ventilation grilles and light fittings — all of these follow from engineering decisions. That is why the designer and the engineer work together from day one: ducts can be hidden in architectural ceiling elements, and grilles can become part of the decor. The dining room then looks intentional rather than a compromise between beauty and technology.

5. In what order are the works done?

  1. measuring and 3D scan
  2. kitchen technology and layout
  3. network design: ventilation, drainage, electrics, low-voltage systems
  4. rough works and laying the networks
  5. floor, walls, ceiling
  6. bar, kitchen, furniture, lighting
  7. commissioning and a test launch

6. What should you not economise on?

Kitchen ventilation, waterproofing of washing areas and spare power capacity. Whatever is saved here will have to be redone in a working venue — with closure and lost revenue.

Questions and answers

When should ventilation be planned in a restaurant?

Before the ceiling design and rough works — right after the kitchen technology. The duct routes determine the ceiling height in the dining room.

Is a grease trap mandatory?

For a venue kitchen, yes: without it the drainage quickly clogs with grease. We determine the type and location in the design.
