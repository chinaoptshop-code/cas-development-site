Turnkey penthouse design and renovation: what to take into account

CAS Development · 25 October 2026

A penthouse is not just a big apartment — it is a property type of its own: panoramic windows, a terrace, complex building services and high expectations for every detail. Here is what to consider before you start, how to build the budget and how to run the project so the result matches the visualisation.

How a penthouse differs from a regular apartment

A penthouse occupies the top floor and often has its own terrace, higher ceilings and floor-to-ceiling glazing. These very advantages make renovation harder: more light means more demands on solar shading and climate control, a terrace means waterproofing, drainage and outdoor lighting, and high ceilings change both the acoustics and the ventilation design.

Logistics is a separate story. Large-format stone, glass partitions and bespoke furniture have to be lifted to the top floor — by goods lift, stairs or crane. We plan this at the design stage, so nobody finds out on site that a slab does not fit into the lift.

  • panoramic glazing: solar shading, motorised curtains, winter condensation
  • terrace: waterproofing, falls, drains, outdoor lights and sockets
  • high ceilings: acoustics, ventilation, access to light fittings
  • delivering large-format materials and furniture to the top floor

Penthouse interior design: where to start

A good design does not start with choosing tiles but with how you live. How many people live there permanently, whether you entertain, whether you need a study, a walk-in wardrobe, a guest bedroom, a wine cabinet or a home cinema. The answers drive the layout, and the layout drives services, lighting and furniture.

Next come the measured survey, space planning, 3D visualisations of key rooms and working drawings: electrics, plumbing, ceilings, floors, wall elevations, specifications for materials and furniture. For a penthouse the drawings are especially important, because most solutions are non-standard: concealed doors, lit niches, floor-to-ceiling built-in wardrobes, glass partitions.

Building services: invisible, but felt every day

In a penthouse the building services are more complex than in a typical apartment, and this is where mistakes cost the most. A wrong wall colour can be repainted in a day; reworking ventilation after finishing means taking ceilings down.

  • heat-recovery ventilation and air conditioning with concealed units
  • underfloor heating by the panoramic windows to avoid a cold zone
  • electrics with spare capacity and dedicated circuits for the kitchen, sauna and terrace
  • low-voltage systems: Wi-Fi across the floor, CCTV, lighting and curtain control
  • water treatment and leak protection with automatic shut-off

All these systems are designed before the renovation starts. The estimate is then accurate and builders work from drawings rather than verbal agreements.

The terrace: a project within the project

A penthouse terrace is a full outdoor room and deserves the same attention as the living room. First we check what the building allows: floor loading, the state of the roof, where the drainage runs and whether structures can be fixed to the parapet. That decides whether you can have a pergola, planters with trees, a hot tub or an outdoor kitchen.

Then the scenarios: morning coffee for two, dinner for eight, a lounge by the fire, shade during the day. Each needs its own furniture, lighting and sockets, plus storage for cushions and textiles in winter. Outdoor furniture is chosen in materials that withstand sun and moisture: aluminium, teak, outdoor fabrics.

  • waterproofing and falls to drains — before the surface is laid
  • outdoor lighting: wall lights, plant uplighting, bollards
  • sockets and water for irrigation and an outdoor kitchen
  • protection from wind and sun: pergola, awnings, parasols

Lighting, storage and materials

With large windows there is enough daylight in a penthouse, so the main task is evening lighting. We usually combine track systems, concealed LED lighting in niches and ceilings, bedside wall lights and decorative pendants over the table. Each zone gets several scenes: task, evening, night.

Storage is designed to disappear: floor-to-ceiling wardrobes flush with the wall, a walk-in dressing room, concealed doors, niches in the corridor. The fewer open surfaces with clutter, the calmer the space — and in a penthouse calm and air are the real luxury.

Materials are chosen to last for years: natural stone or large-format porcelain, veneer, solid wood, brass, quality paint. Approve them from samples in daylight in the room itself — colours on screen and in reality can differ significantly.

How much a turnkey penthouse design and renovation costs

Only an estimate for the actual project gives an exact figure, but you can get a benchmark by multiplying our base rates by the floor area. For a 210 m² penthouse:

  • interior design: from $30 × 210 m² = from $6,300
  • turnkey renovation: from $550 × 210 m² = from $115,500
  • furnishing and procurement: from $250 × 210 m² = from $52,500

In total — from $174,300. This is the lower bound: premium materials, bespoke furniture, complex services and the terrace raise the budget. If CAS carries out the renovation to our design, we keep the final cost within ±5% of the estimate, because one team runs the design, estimate and works.

Running the renovation so the result matches the visualisation

The most common problem with high-end renovations is the gap between design and execution. The crew simplifies details, the supplier offers an equivalent, and the client finds out when it is too late. Three things prevent this: complete working drawings, an estimate with specific materials and constant control on site.

  1. design and working drawings approved before works start
  2. an estimate with line items, quantities and specific materials
  3. a works and delivery schedule: what we order now and what after rough works
  4. photo reports from site every 1–2 weeks
  5. inspection of concealed works before walls and ceilings are closed
  6. procurement, furniture installation and final styling

Furniture, lighting and sanitary ware with long lead times are ordered in parallel with rough works. For example, delivery from our catalogue Ozdoba (in Ukrainian) takes 35–70 days — order after finishing and the penthouse will stand empty.

Mistakes that cost the most in a penthouse

  • starting demolition before the design is finished — then redoing electrics and plumbing
  • not checking how stone, glass and large furniture will get to the top floor
  • forgetting solar shading for panoramic windows — the living room overheats in summer
  • ordering furniture after finishing and waiting for delivery in an empty space
  • economising on concealed works: waterproofing, ventilation, electrics

All these mistakes share one cause: decisions made on the go, without drawings or a schedule. That is why we start with the design and estimate, and only then with construction works.

Questions and answers

How much does penthouse interior design cost?

From $30 per m², with a minimum project fee of $2,500. For a 210 m² penthouse that is from $6,300. We confirm the exact fee after the brief and survey.

Can a penthouse be renovated without a design project?

Technically yes, but it is risky: complex services, non-standard details and expensive materials without drawings lead to rework. The design pays for itself by making the estimate accurate.

How accurate is the estimate?

If CAS carries out the renovation to our design, we keep the final cost within ±5% of the estimate. Changes are possible only if you change the brief or the materials.

Do you give a warranty on the renovation?

Yes, completed works carry a 12-month warranty.
