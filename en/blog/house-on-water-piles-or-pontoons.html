A house on the water: piles or pontoons, and where to start

CAS Development · 7 November 2026

A house over the water stands on piles or pontoons and is a favourite format for holiday resorts and private plots by a lake or river. Here is what it stands on, how the services are brought in and which step to start with. On our website you can scroll through all 12 stages in the “House on the water” scene.

Where do you start building a house on the water?

The first step is a depth survey and a study of the bottom: depth, soil, water-level fluctuations and winter ice. These decide what the house will stand on and how much the foundation will cost. Before the design it is also worth clarifying permits for the water body and the shore — together with a lawyer and the local authorities.

Piles or pontoons: which suits a house on the water?

  • piles — the house stands still; suited to a stable water level and a firm bottom
  • pontoons — the house floats and rises with the water; suited to deep water and water bodies with level changes
  • in both cases the foundation is costed separately, in the estimate, after the survey

How is a house on the water connected to the shore?

A gangway connects the house to the shore: it goes in after the frame and decking on the foundation. The gangway is not only the entrance but also the route for the services: water, sewerage and electricity come from the shore along it.

How do you bring water, sewerage and power from the shore?

Water, sewerage and electricity are run from the shore in protected channels. Wastewater is never discharged into the water: it is taken ashore to a treatment unit or a holding tank. Electrics are moisture-protected, with residual-current devices on every circuit.

What are the frame, roof and facade built from?

Lightness matters for a house on the water: most often it is a frame structure with moisture-resistant materials, a ventilated facade and large glazing facing the water. The deck over the water is the heart of the house, so it goes into the design from the start.

What size can a house on the water be?

A house on the water can range from a compact resort cabin to a full family home — in the scene on our website you can choose 60, 100 or 150 m². For holiday resorts, houses on the water are often combined with cabins on the shore and a shared area — you can see this in the interactive “Holiday resort” site plan.

Which materials withstand constant humidity?

A house on the water needs materials that withstand constant humidity, wind and temperature swings: the structure under the decking is protected against rot and corrosion, the decking is a terrace board that is not slippery after rain, and the facade is a ventilated system that dries quickly. Insulation is generous: from below, the house is cooled by water and wind more than a house on land.

Winter is a separate issue. If the water freezes, ice presses on piles and pontoons. We account for this in the foundation design and the choice of fixings, and for pontoon houses we plan how to protect them from the ice.

Lighting and the evening scene

A house on the water looks its best in the evening, when the light reflects in the water. Lighting for the deck, gangway and paths is built into the design from the start: it means safety for guests in the dark and the key shot for a resort's social media photos.

How long does it take to build a house on the water?

Timescales depend on the foundation, size and season: work on the water is tied to the weather and the water level, so we plan the schedule around those windows. Exact timescales are fixed in the contract after the design and the bottom survey.

How much does a house on the water cost?

A shell-and-core house starts from $600/m², turnkey from $1,100/m². The foundation (piles or pontoons) and the services from the shore are costed separately, in the estimate, because they depend on the depth, the bottom and the distance to the shore.

Questions and answers

Which is better for a house on the water — piles or pontoons?

Piles suit a stable water level and a firm bottom, pontoons suit deep water and level changes. We choose after the depth survey.

How much does a house on the water cost?

Shell and core from $600/m², turnkey from $1,100/m². The foundation and services are costed in the estimate.

How is the sewerage handled?

Wastewater goes ashore to a treatment unit or holding tank; nothing is discharged into the water.

Do you build houses on the water for holiday resorts?

Yes, houses on the water are part of our turnkey resort projects — together with the site plan and cabins on the shore.
