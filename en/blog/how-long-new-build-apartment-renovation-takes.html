How long a new-build apartment renovation takes: what the timing depends on

CAS Development · 1 November 2026

How long a new-build renovation takes depends on more than the area. The timing is made up of consecutive stages, technological pauses and deliveries of materials and built-in furniture. We look at what it consists of, what most often delays the works and what can be done before the start to move in on time.

How long does a new-build apartment renovation take?

The honest answer: as long as the stages of your particular renovation take, put in the right order. A cosmetic refresh of a white-shell apartment and a major renovation from bare concrete with a relocated kitchen are different projects with different schedules. At CAS Development work timing is fixed in the contract and starts from 2 weeks for the simplest scopes; for a full turnkey renovation we draw up the schedule together with the estimate after the design.

To keep the timing from being a surprise, it helps to understand what it consists of. Then it is easier to judge whether a contractor's schedule is realistic and to plan moving, renting or selling another home.

What does the renovation timeline consist of?

A new-build renovation is a chain in which each stage relies on the previous one. Some works can run in parallel, but most cannot.

  1. Design project and estimate: layout, drawings, specifications, schedule.
  2. Preparation: agreements with the management company, delivery of materials, protection of windows and the entrance door.
  3. Partitions per the design, if the layout changes.
  4. Services: electrics, plumbing, ventilation and air conditioning, underfloor heating.
  5. Rough works: plaster, screed, waterproofing of wet zones.
  6. Finishing works: tiles, floors, ceilings, painting, doors.
  7. Installing sanitaryware, light fittings, the kitchen and built-in furniture.
  8. Cleaning, system checks and acceptance.

Each stage is covered in more detail in our article on turnkey apartment renovation stages. Here we focus on what affects duration.

What affects the renovation timing most?

The apartment's condition: bare concrete or white shell

A bare-concrete apartment needs the full cycle: partitions, plaster, screed, all services. A white shell shortens part of the rough works, but only if the existing base suits your design. If sockets are in the wrong places, the screed is uneven or the plumbing does not fit the chosen kitchen, part of the developer's work has to be redone and the time gain disappears.

Scope and complexity of the solutions

Area affects timing less than it seems. Complex details matter much more: concealed doors, shadow gaps, multi-level ceilings, lighting, large-format tiles, stone. Each such solution needs precision and often separate specialists and extra checks.

How ready the design is at the start

If the renovation starts without working drawings, the crew regularly stops to wait for decisions: where the sockets go, how high the worktop is, how the ceiling runs. Each pause costs days and sometimes rework. A complete apartment design project removes most of these questions before the first hammer drill is switched on.

Materials and furniture with long lead times

Made-to-order tiles, sanitaryware from a particular collection, flush-mounted doors, a kitchen and wardrobes to custom dimensions — all have their own production and delivery times. Furniture from China takes 35–70 days, so it is ordered in advance, in parallel with construction.

Approvals in the residential complex

New buildings often have management company rules: hours for noisy works, the procedure for delivering materials, use of the goods lift, waste removal. They should be clarified before the start, because restrictions directly affect the crew's daily productivity.

Season, heating and humidity

Wet processes depend on conditions in the apartment. In a new building where heating is not yet on or windows are not fully sealed, plaster and screed dry differently than in a warm, ventilated room. This is no reason to postpone the renovation, but it is a reason to plan temporary heating, ventilation and humidity control so that finishing works do not have to be shifted.

Decisions the client makes

Part of the timing depends on you: how quickly tile samples, paint colours, light fittings and hardware are approved. If the schedule contains dates for key decisions, the crew does not stand idle and you do not choose in a rush at the last moment. It is convenient to gather all choices into one specification at the design stage.

Which technological pauses cannot be rushed?

Some processes have their own rhythm, and trying to outpace it almost always ends in defects. Screed and plaster must dry, waterproofing must cure, filler must dry before painting. How long this takes depends on the material, layer thickness, humidity and temperature in the apartment, so the exact pauses are put into the schedule according to the technology of the specific materials.

  • tiles are not laid on wet screed — otherwise they may crack or come loose
  • parquet and engineered boards are not installed while humidity is high after wet processes
  • walls are painted after all dirty works so they do not have to be repainted
  • built-in furniture is measured after finishing works, when walls and floor are in their final geometry

A good schedule does not shorten these pauses but fills them with other works: while the screed dries in one zone, the crew works in another or installs services that do not depend on the floor. That is why the schedule should be discussed with the contractor stage by stage, not just as one final date.

How can you shorten the timeline without losing quality?

  • start the design project before you get the keys — from the developer's plan
  • approve the materials specification before the start and order long-lead items right away
  • agree changes in writing and as early as possible — rework always takes longer than a new decision
  • agree material deliveries and noisy works with the management company in advance
  • choose one contractor for the whole cycle so you do not wait for the next crew to become free
  • do not postpone decisions on the kitchen and wardrobes: they are designed together with the services

The kitchen and wardrobes are a separate story. If custom kitchens and wardrobes are designed at the same time as the electrics and plumbing, the outlets are exactly where the appliances go, and production can start right after the check measurement.

Checklist: what to do before the renovation starts

  • get the apartment plan and service diagrams from the developer
  • order the design project and agree the layout with the whole family
  • get the estimate and a stage schedule with dates for key decisions
  • find out the complex's rules on renovation, lift use, waste and quiet hours
  • choose the kitchen, appliances and sanitaryware — the services are prepared for them
  • decide which furniture will be made to order and which will be ready-made
  • agree how you will receive reports and approve changes

Why are renovations most often delayed?

  • the renovation started without drawings and decisions are made on the go
  • the client changes the layout after the services have been installed
  • tiles or sanitaryware ran out of stock and a replacement is sought during the works
  • the kitchen was ordered only after finishing works, although it should have been designed together with the electrics
  • several independent contractors work on site and wait for each other
  • concealed works were not checked in time and a defect was found under the tiles

Most of these causes are removed at the preparation stage: the design, specification, estimate and decision schedule give the crew a clear sequence and you clear dates when your choice is needed.

What should you check in the contract about timing?

A deadline given verbally guarantees nothing. The contract should include a stage schedule, payments tied to completed stages, the procedure for approving changes and how timing changes if the client alters the design or delays choosing materials. More on the contract, warranty and payment page.

It is also useful to agree the reporting format. Regular photo reports show whether work is on schedule before any delay becomes visible. For those who cannot visit the site often there is online renovation monitoring with a 24/7 camera for an extra fee.

Can you move in in parts?

Sometimes you want to move in earlier, finishing only the kitchen, bathroom and bedroom. In an apartment this is rarely justified: dust and noise spread across the whole area, and it is harder for the crew to work when people live there and furniture stands around. It is more realistic to plan the move after cleaning and acceptance, and to install built-in furniture that arrives later in the clean apartment on a separate schedule.

Questions and answers

How long does a new-build apartment renovation take?

It depends on the apartment's condition, the scope of works and the complexity of the solutions. At CAS timing is fixed in the contract and starts from 2 weeks for simple scopes; for a turnkey renovation we draw up the schedule after the design together with the estimate.

Is a renovation faster in a white-shell apartment?

It can be, if the existing base suits your design. If sockets, screed or plumbing have to be redone, the time gain disappears.

What most often delays a renovation?

Starting without a design, changing decisions during the works, ordering long-lead materials and furniture late, and management company restrictions that were not known in advance.

When should the kitchen and wardrobes be ordered?

Design them together with the services so the outlets match the appliances. Produce them after the check measurement, when finishing works are complete.

Can the design project start before I get the keys?

Yes, from the developer's plan. After you receive the keys, check measurements are taken and the drawings refined — this saves time at the start of the renovation.
