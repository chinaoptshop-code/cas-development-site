Garden lighting and path paving

CAS Development · 18 November 2026

Paths and lights are what you use on your plot every day. We explain how to plan garden lighting and path paving together, which materials to choose, what to light and why the cable must go into the ground before the paving.

Routes first, material second

Paths go where people actually walk: from the gate to the front door, from the terrace to the barbecue area, from the parking to the house, to the utility area and the sauna. If a path is laid to look “pretty” rather than practical, a trodden track will appear across the lawn.

  • main path — straight and wide enough for two people to pass
  • parking and driveway — a base built for the weight of a car
  • garden paths — can be narrower, with stepping stones
  • around the pool and terrace — a non-slip surface
  • access to manholes, meters and the septic tank — not under a flowerbed

It is handy to check routes on the site plan together with the lighting: every path used in the evening should be lit, and every step or change in level visible in the dark. If the plot slopes, paths follow the contours or become steps with landings, and water from the paving is led into channels or onto the lawn, not towards the house.

Path paving: which materials to choose

The material is chosen for the load, the style of the house and the budget. For the driveway strength matters; for garden paths it is the look and feel underfoot.

  • concrete pavers — a practical option for most paths and hard areas
  • large-format slabs — a modern look, combine well with grass in the joints
  • natural stone — durable, for main routes and terraces
  • decking boards — for decks by the house and pool
  • gravel and fine aggregate — for far paths and areas that do not need a rigid base

The durability of paving is determined not by the slabs but by the base beneath them: a prepared layer of crushed stone, compaction, a fall for water and an edging along the side. Without edging, the slabs drift apart over time and the lawn creeps into the joints.

Landscape design and garden landscaping

Garden lighting: what to light and why

Garden lighting has three jobs: safety, convenience and atmosphere. First you light where people walk and could trip, then what you want to enjoy in the evening.

  • paths — low bollards or fittings set into the paving
  • steps and level changes — lighting for every step
  • entrance, gates and parking — even light without glare
  • trees and shrubs — in-ground uplights
  • terrace and seating area — warm, soft light
  • pool and ponds — underwater lighting

Fittings are chosen with protection against moisture and dust, and the light is warm so the garden does not look like a car park in the evening. A few quiet accents are better than a floodlight over the whole yard.

Why the cable goes in before the paving

Lighting cables, ducts under paths and lines to the gates are laid before paving and lawn, together with drainage and irrigation. If you decide to add lighting after the slabs are down, you will have to lift them or run wires over the ground.

  1. In the design — a layout of fittings and lines, power points.
  2. During earthworks — cable in a protective duct, sleeves under every path.
  3. After paving and planting — installing the fittings.
  4. At the end — setting up scenes: evening, night, party.

Lighting is easy to control by schedule, by a daylight sensor or from a smartphone. Separate groups — paths, trees, terrace — let you switch on only what you need. You can see how the cables run under the lawn in X-ray mode of the “Landscape from scratch” animation on the landscape design page.

What lighting and paving cost

The lighting layout, path plan and paving drawings are part of the landscape design project — from $50 per 100 m². The works are costed in the estimate: the price depends on the paved area, the material, the number of fittings and the length of the lines. We give the exact amount after inspecting the plot and agreeing the design.

If the budget is limited, paving and lighting can be done in phases. First the main paths, the driveway and lighting at the entrance, while cables and ducts for future lines are laid across the whole plot straight away. Then garden paths and lighting for trees and the terrace are added later without lifting anything.

Questions and answers

What comes first: paving or lighting?

Cables and ducts first, then paving, and the fittings are installed at the end. That way you will not have to lift the slabs.

What kind of light suits a garden?

Warm and soft, with accents on paths, steps, trees and the terrace. Fittings protected against moisture and dust.

Does a path need edging?

Yes. It holds the edge of the paving and the lawn, and the slabs do not drift apart over time.

How much does path paving cost?

It depends on the area, material and base. We cost it in the estimate after an inspection; paving drawings are part of the design from $50 per 100 m².
