Landscape design for a plot: where to start and how much it costs

CAS Development · 16 November 2026

A good garden starts not with choosing plants but with terrain, water and routes. We explain where to start landscape design for a plot, what the project includes, in what order the works go and how much the project costs.

Where do you start landscape design for a plot?

Start with how you want to live on the plot, not with pictures of gardens. Where you will have breakfast in summer, where the children will play, where the car will stand, whether you need a pool, a sauna, a vegetable garden, a barbecue spot. These answers define the zoning, and materials and plants are then chosen to fit it.

  • who uses the plot and how often: a permanent home or a weekend house
  • how much time you are ready to spend looking after the garden
  • which zones are needed: terrace, rest area, playground, parking, utility area
  • what is already there: trees you want to keep, structures, a fence
  • the budget and the sequence: what to do now and what later

Landscape works best when it is planned together with the house: the windows face the right views, the terrace is on the right side, and services do not have to be dug up again. If the house is still at the design stage, the garden should be built into it from the start.

What does a site survey assess?

The first visit assesses what you will be working with. We look at the terrain and slopes, the soil type, existing planting, where water stands after rain, where the wind comes from and how the house and neighbouring trees cast shade during the day.

Next comes a topographic survey: plot boundaries, levels, the position of the house and services. The project is then built on the real plot rather than on a cadastral sketch. Without it, it is easy to get the slopes wrong, and water will go not into the drainage but under the terrace or into the basement.

Landscape design and garden landscaping

What does a landscape design project include?

  • a master plan and zoning of the plot with paths, terraces and rest areas
  • 3D visualisations so you can see the garden before work starts
  • grading: slopes, retaining walls, water drainage
  • a planting plan and plant selection to suit the soil, light and your maintenance
  • drainage, automatic irrigation and outdoor lighting layouts
  • drawings of paving, kerbs, the terrace, a pool or barbecue area as per the brief

The level of detail depends on the task. For a small plot by the house, a master plan, a planting plan and service layouts are enough. For a large site or a holiday resort, access roads, parking, path lighting and a phasing plan are added.

In what order is a plot landscaped?

  1. Survey and setting out the zones according to the project.
  2. Grading: levelling and falls away from the house.
  3. Drainage: trenches with geotextile, gravel and a pipe falling to a well.
  4. Laying irrigation and lighting cables — before paving and lawn.
  5. Kerbs, paths and the terrace.
  6. Fertile soil, lawn, planting trees, shrubs and flower beds.
  7. Installing lights and setting up the irrigation.

The order matters: everything underground — drainage, irrigation, cables — is done before paving and lawn. Otherwise finished paths will have to be taken up. You can see how this goes step by step in the “Landscape from scratch” animation on the landscape design page: choose the area and style and switch on the X-ray to see drainage, irrigation and cables under the lawn.

How do you get plants and maintenance right?

Plants are chosen for a specific spot: sun or shade, moist or dry soil, wind. The second question is how much time you are ready to give the garden. If the house is for weekends, it is better to have fewer demanding flower beds and more lawn, shrubs and conifers, with automatic irrigation.

  • do not plant large trees closer to the house and services than their future crown allows
  • leave room for maintenance: access to wells, meters, the septic tank
  • plan evening lighting together with the paths — the cable is laid before paving
  • protect the lawn next to the terrace and pool from spreading with kerbs

How much does landscape design cost?

A landscape design project at CAS Development starts from $50 per 100 m². For a plot of 1,000 m² (10 × 100 m²) the project costs from 10 × $50 = $500. We name the exact sum after inspecting the plot: it depends on the area, terrain and the set of drawings.

Landscaping works — paving, terraces, drainage, irrigation, lighting, planting — are costed separately after the project is approved, per estimate. Here the cost depends on the area, terrain and chosen materials, so a single rate per 100 m² says nothing about the works.

Questions and answers

How much does landscape design for a plot cost?

The project starts from $50 per 100 m². Landscaping works are costed separately per estimate after the plot inspection and project approval.

When is the best time to order landscape design?

Together with the house design or before any work on the plot starts. Then drainage, irrigation and lighting cables are laid before paving and lawn.

Do you carry out the landscaping, not just the design?

Yes, we design and landscape the plot turnkey: drainage, irrigation, lighting, paving, terraces, lawn and planting.

Is a topographic survey needed?

Yes. It gives boundaries, levels and the position of services — without it, it is easy to get slopes and water drainage wrong.
