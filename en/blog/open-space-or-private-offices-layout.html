Open space or private offices: planning an office around your team

CAS Development · 26 October 2026

Open space or private offices — most often a mixed format wins: open workstations for teams plus closed rooms for specific tasks. Here is how to choose a layout that fits how your team really works rather than fashion.

Where do you start choosing an office layout?

Start with how your team works: an office layout answers the question “how do we work”. How many people spend most of the day on headphones and calls, who works in pairs and teams, how often clients visit, where meetings happen. Without these answers the choice between open space and private offices becomes a matter of taste, and then the team spends months adapting to an awkward space.

  • how many people are in the office every day and how many work hybrid
  • which departments talk all the time and which disturb each other
  • how many calls and video meetings there are per day
  • whether you receive clients and where
  • how the team will grow in the near future

When is open space the right choice?

An open space works well for teams that need fast communication: product and IT teams, marketing, sales. It is easier to rearrange for a new structure, everyone gets more daylight, and the renovation itself is simpler — fewer partitions and doors.

But an open space without well-planned acoustics and places for calls quickly becomes noisy. That is why for IT companies we plan not only rows of desks but also quiet zones, booths and meeting rooms — more on the open-space office design page.

When does an office need private offices?

Private offices make sense where you need quiet, confidentiality or a separate space for meetings: management, lawyers, finance, HR. Separate rooms are also needed for technical tasks — a server room, archive, storage. The downside of a cellular layout is more partitions, doors and separate lighting and ventilation lines, which means more construction work.

Open space or private offices: why does a mixed layout win?

A mixed format wins because the office stays flexible and people have somewhere to go for a call or focused work: open workstations for teams plus closed rooms for specific tasks.

  • open workstations grouped by team
  • a quiet zone away from the kitchen and entrance
  • booths for calls and video meetings
  • meeting rooms of different sizes — from a room for two to a presentation hall
  • private offices only where they are really needed

What makes an open office comfortable?

An open office becomes comfortable not because of the number of walls but because of the details: acoustic panels on walls and ceiling, soft floor finishes, screens between desks, even glare-free light on monitors. We build these solutions into the design project before the renovation — reworking them later costs more. Ready-made booths and acoustic panels can be ordered directly from factories in China.

Which meeting and rest zones does an office need?

Besides workstations, an office needs a kitchen, a lounge and an area for informal talks: they take the load off meeting rooms and let people rest without leaving the office. These zones are best placed closer to the entrance and away from the quiet zone, so that coffee conversations do not disturb those doing focused work.

We plan storage separately: document cabinets, personal lockers, a cloakroom. When storage is not planned, boxes and folders quickly fill the aisles, and even a well-planned open space looks cluttered.

How does the layout affect the renovation budget?

An open space usually needs less construction work but more acoustics, networks and furniture, while private offices add partitions and doors. Both fit within the same rates: an office renovation at CAS starts from $350/m² (works and rough materials); a design project starts from $30/m², with a minimum of $2,500. Questions and answers

Which is better for an office — open space or private offices?

It depends on how the team works. A mixed layout usually wins: open workstations for teams plus meeting rooms, call booths and a quiet zone.

How do you make an open space quiet?

Acoustic panels on walls and ceiling, soft floor finishes, screens between desks, call booths and soundproofed meeting rooms — we build all of this into the design.

Which is cheaper to renovate — open space or private offices?

An open space usually needs fewer partitions and doors but more acoustics and networks. The itemised estimate shows the exact difference.

Can the layout be changed without a new renovation?

If flexible zones and networks with spare capacity are planned at the design stage — yes, workstations can be rearranged without construction work.

What does an open-space office design include?

Workstation zoning, meeting rooms and quiet zones, acoustics, lighting and building services, 3D visuals and working drawings. Office design starts from $30/m², office renovation from $350/m² (works and rough materials).
