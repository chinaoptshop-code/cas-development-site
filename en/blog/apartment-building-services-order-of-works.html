Apartment building services in a turnkey renovation: electrics, plumbing, ventilation, underfloor heating

CAS Development · 1 November 2026

Building services are the part of a renovation you cannot see after handover, yet they decide whether the breaker trips, whether you flood the neighbours and whether mould appears in the bathroom. We explain the order in which electrics, plumbing, ventilation and underfloor heating are installed, which solutions are standard market practice and what to check while the walls are still unplastered.

Apartment building services: why the order matters more than the brands

An expensive mixer tap will not save you from a leak in a badly made joint, and good cable will not save an overloaded circuit. Most problems with services come not from materials but from the wrong sequence and missing checks before things are covered up. That is why services in a turnkey renovation follow a design and a clear order: decisions on paper first, then installation, then testing, and only after that plaster, screed and finishes.

  1. services design and decisions on appliances
  2. demolition, partitions, checking risers and incoming supplies
  3. first-fix electrics and low-voltage cabling
  4. water supply, drainage, concealed frames
  5. ventilation, air conditioning, pipe runs and condensate drainage
  6. testing: pressure tests, measurements, photo records, sign-off certificates
  7. plaster, screed with underfloor heating, waterproofing of wet areas
  8. finishes, then second-fix electrics and plumbing

Stage 0. Design: what to decide before the first chase

Sockets, switches and water outlets are tied to furniture and appliances, so you first need the layout and furniture plan. The kitchen, washing machine, water heater, air conditioners and water treatment should be chosen at least in terms of size and power: cable sizes, circuits in the consumer unit, heights and positions of outlets depend on them.

  • a socket and switch plan with heights and positions
  • a diagram of lighting circuits and control scenes
  • a single-line diagram of the consumer unit with breaker and RCD ratings
  • a water and drainage diagram with outlet heights
  • a diagram of ventilation, air-conditioning runs and condensate drainage
  • an underfloor heating layout and thermostat positions

In a new-build, you first check what the developer has done: where the floor distribution board is, what power is allocated to the apartment, how the risers and ventilation ducts are brought out. More on the specifics on the new-build renovation page.

Electrics

Consumer unit and circuits

The consumer unit is the heart of the electrics. Typically it contains a main switch, residual-current devices, circuit breakers by group and, if needed, a voltage relay or surge protection. Circuits are split so that a fault in one zone does not cut power to the whole apartment, and heavy loads have their own lines.

  • lighting — separate from sockets, by zones of the apartment
  • sockets — by room, with the kitchen worktop area on its own circuit
  • dedicated lines for the hob, oven, dishwasher, washing machine, water heater, air conditioners and underfloor heating
  • residual-current protection for socket circuits and wet areas — market practice is a 30 mA rating
  • a label on every breaker and a diagram on the consumer unit door

Cable and routing

Copper three-core cable is used — line, neutral and protective earth. Market practice is 1.5 mm² for lighting, 2.5 mm² for sockets, and for the hob and other heavy loads a size calculated from the power, usually 4–6 mm². In walls, cable runs strictly vertically and horizontally in defined zones so it is not drilled through later; in floors and ceilings it goes in flexible conduit. Junction boxes must remain accessible.

Low-voltage systems

Internet, Wi-Fi, intercom, cameras, leak sensors and smart home are installed at the same time as the power cabling but along separate routes. As a rule, a communications cable runs from the incoming board to a media cabinet, and from there twisted pair goes to the TV, the workstation and ceiling Wi-Fi access points. For a smart home, the design stage decides which circuits will be controlled and leaves space for modules in the consumer unit or back boxes.

Bathroom

Stricter rules apply in the bathroom: sockets and switches are placed outside splash zones, light fittings have the appropriate ingress protection, and metal parts — pipes, bath, shower tray — are connected by equipotential bonding. Underfloor heating and the towel rail are connected through residual-current protection.

What to check before plastering

  • sockets and switches match the plan for heights and positions
  • a photo of every wall with a tape measure — so you know later where the cable runs
  • insulation resistance measurement and a check that the residual-current protection trips
  • a protective earth conductor in every socket

Plumbing and drainage

Water supply: manifold or tee system

With a manifold system, every point — mixer, toilet, washing machine — is fed by its own pipe from the manifold and can be shut off without turning off the whole apartment. A tee system is simpler and cheaper but less convenient to maintain. Pipes are welded polypropylene, PEX or multilayer pipe with press fittings; only permanent joints are left inside walls and floors. Cold water is insulated against condensation, hot water against heat loss.

  • at the inlet: valves, coarse and fine filters, a pressure reducer, meters as required by the building
  • a leak protection system — sensors and motorised valves
  • access to the manifold, valves and filters through access panels

Drainage

Typically, 50 mm pipes are used for the basin, shower and washing machine, and 110 mm for the toilet. Horizontal runs have a constant fall towards the stack — market practice is about 2–3 cm per metre for 50 mm pipes, with no back-falls. Bends are made with two 45° elbows rather than one sharp one, and access points are left at junctions. The building's common stack is not moved or reduced without approval, and it is wrapped in acoustic insulation for quiet.

Concealed frames, drains and waterproofing

A wall-hung toilet and basin are fixed to concealed frames — steel frames installed before the walls are boarded. A walk-in shower with a linear or point drain needs a fall in the screed and an exact drain height. Wet areas are covered with liquid or sheet waterproofing turned up the walls, in the shower to the full splash height; corners and pipe penetrations are sealed with tapes and collars.

What to check before covering up

  • pressure testing of pipework at the pressure and duration required by the pipe manufacturer or the design, with a photo of the gauge
  • outlet heights and positions according to the diagram
  • drainage falls checked with a level, no sagging
  • continuous waterproofing, including corners and around the drain

Ventilation and air conditioning

In an apartment block, extraction from the kitchen and bathrooms usually goes through ventilation ducts. They must not be blocked, reduced or used for other purposes: the ventilation of both your apartment and your neighbours' depends on them. The cooker hood is connected according to the design and building rules, and the bathroom gets a fan with a non-return valve. Airtight windows let almost no air in, so supply air is needed: window or wall vents, decentralised heat-recovery units where the facade allows, or a central supply-and-extract system with heat recovery if there is space above the ceiling.

Air-conditioning runs — insulated refrigerant pipes, condensate drain and cable — are laid before plastering. The drain needs a fall or a condensate pump, and the position of the outdoor unit is agreed according to the building rules. Once the walls are closed, the route cannot be changed without destroying the finishes.

  • supply air to living rooms and extraction from the kitchen, bathrooms and walk-in wardrobes
  • air transfer between rooms — a gap under the doors or grilles
  • indoor units placed so the airflow does not blow onto the bed or desk
  • access to filters and drainage for servicing

Underfloor heating

In apartments, electric underfloor heating is most common: cable in the screed, a thin mat in the tile adhesive, or film under laminate or parquet if the flooring manufacturer allows it. Water underfloor heating from central heating in an apartment block is usually not permitted because it upsets the balance of the system. It is possible with individual heating — via a mixing unit and to a design.

  • a dedicated line with a breaker and residual-current protection
  • a floor temperature sensor in a conduit — so it can be replaced
  • insulation under the screed so you do not heat the neighbours below
  • not under the kitchen, wardrobes, bath or other furniture without legs
  • cable resistance measured before and after laying, a photo of the layout with dimensions

How services affect ceiling heights and floor levels

Building services take up centimetres, and it is worth knowing this before you fall in love with a particular ceiling height. Supply-and-extract ductwork and ducted air-conditioning units need space above a suspended ceiling. A screed with underfloor heating and insulation is thicker than a standard one, and a walk-in shower needs room for the drain and the fall. Concealed frames for wall-hung sanitaryware and manifolds take up niches in the walls.

That is why design and services in the project must be coordinated: where the ceiling drops, where boxing hides services, what the floor levels are in different rooms and how to make the transitions without thresholds.

Documents and standards: what you should receive

Work is carried out in line with current building codes, electrical installation rules and equipment manufacturers' requirements. For you as the client, what matters more than the list of regulations is the documents left after the renovation. You will need them when, a few years later, you want to hang a shelf, replace the water heater or find a leak.

  • as-built diagrams of electrics and plumbing — how things were actually installed
  • photos of walls and floors with a tape measure before closing up
  • a single-line diagram of the consumer unit with labelled circuits
  • electrical test reports and pipework pressure-test records
  • data sheets and warranty documents for the equipment

If the renovation is done by a contractor you do not fully trust, concealed works can be checked by an independent specialist — see the article on technical supervision.

Common mistakes in apartment services

  • sockets left “as the developer put them”, not tied to furniture and appliances — then come the extension leads
  • one circuit for the whole kitchen, heavy appliances without dedicated lines
  • diagonal cable runs in walls
  • pipe joints in the screed with no access
  • drainage with no fall or with sharp bends
  • a blocked or reduced ventilation duct, an extractor with no supply air
  • underfloor heating under furniture and without insulation
  • closing walls without pressure tests and photo records

Questions and answers

In what order are services installed in an apartment?

First the design and decisions on appliances, then demolition and partitions, first-fix electrics, water supply and drainage, ventilation and air-conditioning runs, testing and photo records, and only after that plaster, screed and finishes.

What cable size is needed for sockets and lighting?

Market practice is 1.5 mm² copper cable for lighting, 2.5 mm² for sockets, and for the hob and other heavy loads a calculated size, usually 4–6 mm². Exact values are set by the design based on power and line length.

Manifold or tee water distribution — which is better?

A manifold costs more, but each point can be shut off separately and there are no intermediate joints in the walls. A tee system is simpler and cheaper. For apartments with several bathrooms and many appliances, a manifold is usually chosen.

Can water underfloor heating be installed in an apartment?

From central heating in an apartment block — usually not, as it disrupts the building's system. Water underfloor heating is possible with individual heating via a mixing unit. Otherwise electric heating is used.

Can a cooker hood be connected to the ventilation duct?

It depends on the duct design and the building rules. The duct must not be blocked or reduced, and the hood must not cut off natural extraction. The connection option is decided in the design.

What should be checked before plastering?

That sockets and outlets match the plan, photos of walls with a tape measure, insulation measurements and the residual-current protection working, pipework pressure tests, drainage falls and air-conditioning runs.
