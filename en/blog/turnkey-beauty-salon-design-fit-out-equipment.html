Turnkey beauty salon: design, fit-out, equipment

CAS Development · 25 October 2026

A turnkey beauty salon is a space where stylists are comfortable working and clients want to come back. We explain how to plan workstations, what to build into the fit-out, which equipment and furniture to choose, and how to calculate the budget before opening.

Where to start: format and services

Before the design, define clearly which services the salon will offer. Hairdressing, manicure and pedicure, cosmetology, brows and lashes, massage — each service has its own requirements for space, water, ventilation, light and privacy. Both the layout and the fit-out estimate depend on the list of services.

  • the number of workstations for each service
  • which services need separate treatment rooms
  • how many wash basins and where they will go
  • whether a sterilisation room and a staff area are needed
  • the waiting area and reception: how many clients at once
  • the style and mood the client should associate with the brand

If you are still choosing premises, check them against this list before signing the lease: can water and drainage be brought to the wash basins, can ventilation be routed out, is enough electrical power available? A convenient street entrance and a shop window are also part of a salon's marketing.

Workstation layout

A beauty salon layout is a balance between the number of workstations and comfort. A client in the chair should not feel they are sitting in a queue, and a stylist should not have to reach across a colleague to a socket. Between stations you need room for tools, a trolley and a clear passage.

We plan the routes separately. The client comes in, takes off their coat, waits, goes to the chair, goes to the wash basin and back, and pays at reception. Staff move between workstations, the sterilisation room and the back room. It is good when these flows do not cross at pinch points.

Rooms for cosmetology, massage or hair removal are worth separating: privacy, sound insulation and dedicated lighting matter here. Frosted glass partitions help keep a sense of space while screening the treatment from view.

Turnkey commercial fit-out

The salon design project

A salon design project starts with the layout and the workflow, and only then moves to the interior concept. It includes drawings with the position of every workstation, wash basin, socket, switch and light fitting, ventilation and plumbing layouts, 3D visualisations and a specification of materials and furniture.

Lighting is a topic of its own. A stylist needs even, shadow-free light with accurate colour rendering, especially for colouring and make-up. A client in the waiting area needs warm, calm light. So a salon usually combines ambient light, lit mirrors at the workstations and decorative light for atmosphere.

Our design project starts from $30/m², with a minimum project price of $2,500. For a 70 m² salon the rate gives 70 × $30 = $2,100, so the minimum price of $2,500 applies. For a 100 m² salon, from 100 × $30 = $3,000.

Fit-out: what to build into the services

Most problems in beauty salons come from services that were not planned at the start. Moving a wash basin or adding a socket after the finishes means taking up the floor or walls again. That is why services are installed to the drawings before the finishing works.

  • ventilation — essential for colouring, manicure and cosmetology areas
  • dedicated electrical lines for hair dryers, sterilisers and cosmetology equipment
  • sockets at every workstation, allowing for the stylist's tools
  • water supply and drainage to the wash basins and treatment rooms
  • a water heater with spare capacity for simultaneous hair washing
  • floor and wall finishes resistant to dyes, chemicals and frequent cleaning

Salon materials are chosen to withstand daily cleaning with disinfectants. Light surfaces must resist dye stains, and joints must be tight so dirt does not collect in them. More on commercial fit-outs on the commercial premises renovation page.

Reception, waiting area and entrance

The client gets the first impression of the salon while still on the street: the sign, the shop window, a clean and convenient entrance. Then comes the reception, where they are greeted, and the waiting area, where they spend a few minutes before the treatment. This is where the sense of the salon's level is formed, so it is worth investing in expressive materials and lighting here.

The reception desk must be convenient for the administrator: room for a computer and a card terminal, hidden cables, shelves for retail cosmetics. The waiting area needs soft seating, hooks or a wardrobe for coats, space for bags and, if desired, a small coffee station. It is good when a client in the waiting area cannot see other guests' treatments.

  • a sign and shop window visible from the street
  • a reception desk with space for equipment and a cosmetics display
  • a wardrobe for clients' coats
  • soft seating and warm light in the waiting area

Furniture, mirrors and equipment

After the fit-out the salon has to be equipped: styling chairs, wash units, manicure tables, treatment couches, a reception desk, waiting-area furniture, lit mirrors, light fittings, shelving and storage, textiles. Some of these items are cost-effective to bring directly from factories in China.

Items made in China are ordered by specification for the whole project rather than piece by piece. Delivery takes 35–70 days, so we place the order once the specification is approved, in parallel with the fit-out. Professional treatment equipment is usually chosen by the salon owner or the stylists — we allow space, sockets and water for it in the design.

Stages of opening a turnkey salon

  1. brief: services, number of workstations, style, budget
  2. checking the premises: water, drainage, ventilation, power
  3. design project and specification
  4. estimate and schedule
  5. fit-out with services and finishes
  6. equipping: furniture, mirrors, lighting, decor
  7. installation, placement, cleaning and handover

When one team runs all these stages, the schedule shrinks: furniture is ordered while the fit-out is under way, and furniture dimensions match the positions of sockets and water.

Questions and answers

How much does a beauty salon design project cost?

From $30/m², with a minimum project price of $2,500. For a 100 m² salon, from $3,000. The fit-out is costed in the estimate after the design.

What matters most in a beauty salon fit-out?

The services: ventilation, dedicated electrical lines, sockets at every workstation, water and drainage to the wash basins. All of this goes into the design before the finishes.

Do you supply furniture for the salon?

Yes. Furniture, lit mirrors, lighting and decor come directly from factories in China as part of the project procurement; delivery takes 35–70 days.

Can I order only the fit-out to an existing design?

Yes. We carry out the fit-out to your designer's project, after checking the drawings and preparing an estimate.
