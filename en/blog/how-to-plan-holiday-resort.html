How to plan a holiday resort: from idea to first guests

CAS Development · 25 October 2026

A holiday resort is a business where architecture directly affects occupancy and average spend. Here is how to plan a resort and get from idea to launch without unnecessary costs.

1. Format and guest

Who is the resort for: families, couples, corporate groups, anglers? Year-round or seasonal? That determines the number and type of cabins, the restaurant, spa, children's and sports areas. A year-round resort needs insulated houses, heating and winter activities; a seasonal one puts more emphasis on terraces, the beach and open spaces.

Decide early how the resort will make money: accommodation, restaurant, spa, events, boat or equipment hire. That determines which buildings come first and which can wait for the next phase.

It is worth looking at what the region lacks: if there are already resorts for loud groups nearby, a quiet retreat for couples can stand out by format alone.

2. The plot and the resort master plan

  • terrain, water, forest, orientation
  • privacy of the cabins and views from the windows
  • guest and staff routes
  • parking, service yard, utility networks
  • space for future expansion

The master plan is the resort's key document. It shows where each cabin stands, how guests walk from parking to their room, where utilities run and how staff service the site without disturbing guests. A mistake in the master plan is the most expensive to fix later.

We place cabins with the sun and views in mind, and keep machinery, waste and staff access in a separate zone. Guests should feel nature, not logistics.

3. The cabins

A-frame, modular or timber-frame — the choice affects budget, timeline and how recognisable the resort is. Interiors should be cosy and practical: easy-care materials, well-planned storage, good lighting.

  • A-frame — a striking silhouette that works well in photos and on social media
  • modular houses — predictable quality and repeatability
  • timber-frame — flexible layouts and sizes
  • in every option — a terrace, a view from the bed and a comfortable bathroom

Repeatability matters for the business: when cabins share a few standard layouts, they are easier to build, furnish and maintain. Variety comes from sizes, finishes and positions on the site.

4. Restaurant, spa and shared spaces

The restaurant, reception, spa with sauna and hot tubs, beach and jetty are what shape the impression of the resort and bring extra revenue. Shared spaces should be visible from across the site and well connected by paths, while kitchens and service areas stay out of guests' sight.

The resort restaurant can also serve guests who do not stay overnight, so plan a separate entrance and parking for it. The spa can be sold as a stand-alone product for a few hours.

5. Utilities and landscaping

Water, drainage, electricity, heating and site lighting go into the master plan before construction. Landscaping is planned separately: paths, lighting, planting, relaxation areas by the water. Landscape design starts from $50 per 100 m² of site area.

If there are no mains networks nearby, solutions for water, wastewater and energy are built in at the concept stage, because they affect both the budget and the placement of buildings.

6. Launch stages

  1. concept and master plan
  2. architecture and interiors, 3D
  3. working drawings and estimate
  4. construction
  5. furnishing, including sourcing directly from China
  6. preparing to welcome guests

A resort can open in phases: first some of the cabins and the restaurant, then expansion. The first guests bring revenue while the next buildings are under construction.

Furniture, textiles and lighting are ordered in parallel with construction, so cabins are ready for guests as soon as works are complete.

How much a holiday resort costs

Construction starts from $600 per m², a turnkey house from $1,100 per m², interior design from $30 per m². As a benchmark, a 40 m² turnkey cabin costs from $44,000 (40 × $1,100). We quote the total cost of the complex after the brief and master plan: it depends on the number and size of buildings, utilities and landscaping.

For a resort with several cabins, a restaurant and a spa, the budget is made up of separate estimates — for each building, the networks and the landscaping. That shows clearly what can be postponed to the next phase.

Common mistakes when creating a resort

  • building cabins without a master plan, wherever they happen to fit
  • placing houses with windows facing each other
  • not reserving utility capacity for expansion
  • economising on interiors that guests see in photos
  • forgetting the service yard, laundry and staff facilities

All of these are solved on paper — in the master plan and design — far more cheaply than on an already built resort.

Questions and answers

How much does a holiday resort design cost?

It depends on the area and the mix of buildings. Interior design starts from $30 per m²; we quote the cost of the complex after the brief.

Can a resort be launched in phases?

Yes. The master plan can be split into phases: first some cabins and the restaurant, then expansion.

Which cabins are best for a holiday resort?

A-frame, modular or timber-frame — it depends on budget, seasonality and concept. We compare the options at the master plan stage.

How much does a turnkey cabin cost?

From $1,100 per m²; a 40 m² cabin, for example, costs from $44,000. The exact amount comes from the estimate.
