Turnkey timber house: glulam or profiled timber, stages and price

CAS Development · 18 November 2026

People choose a turnkey timber house for the living wood in the interior and a warmth that is hard to get from other materials. We explain how glulam and profiled timber differ, how settling affects the schedule, how construction goes stage by stage and what makes up the price.

Glulam or profiled timber

Glulam is glued together from dried lamellas. It hardly settles or cracks, so finishing can start sooner, and the walls are often left exposed in the interior. It costs more at the start.

Profiled timber is solid wood milled with a profile so that the courses fit tightly. It is cheaper, but once the walls are up the house needs time to settle. Windows, doors, partitions and services are installed with allowance for the fact that the walls will still be moving down.

The choice depends on the budget, the timeline and whether you want to see wood in the interior. We build these decisions into the design before the estimate, because the details and the order of works depend on them.

If the wood stays exposed, the inside of the house has its own subtleties too. You cannot hide cables and pipes in a chase in a wall like this, so we plan their routes in advance: some run in partitions, floors and ceilings, and some are left visible as an element of the interior.

Settling and what it means for the schedule

Settling is a natural property of wood: as it dries, the timber shrinks slightly in height, and the walls with it. For the owner this means two things. The first is the schedule: with profiled timber there will be a pause between the shell and the finishing, and it is worth allowing for it if you are planning a move-in date. The second is the details. Windows and doors are set in sliding frames that let the walls move, and partitions, stairs and pipes are fixed so that they do not get distorted.

How long the pause will last in your case depends on the timber and the season. We state this in the design and in the works schedule.

Timber house

Stages of building a timber house

  1. Topographic survey: mapping the relief and setting out the axes of the house.
  2. Foundation for the soil and loads from the design, with sleeves for services.
  3. Timber walls with inter-course sealing, floor structures.
  4. Rafters and roof covering.
  5. Thermal envelope: windows and doors allowing for settling, roof and floor insulation, protective treatment of the timber.
  6. Services: electrics, heating, water, drainage, ventilation.
  7. Rough and fine finishing, interior doors, sanitaryware, sockets, lighting.
  8. Gutters, soffits, perimeter paving, handover with signed certificates.

You can watch how a house is built step by step in the “House from scratch” animation on the turnkey timber house page.

Shell, thermal envelope and turnkey

  • shell — foundation, timber walls with sealing, floor structures, rafters and roof covering
  • thermal envelope — windows and entrance doors, roof and floor insulation, treatment of the timber, gutters and soffits; priced by estimate
  • turnkey — the shell and thermal envelope, building services, finishes, doors, sanitaryware, sockets and lighting

Priced separately, by estimate, are furniture and appliances, connection to external utilities, a borehole and a septic tank. How these options differ in terms of risk is covered in the article turnkey house or shell.

How much a timber house costs

Our rates: the shell from $600/m², turnkey with services and finishes from $1,100/m², architectural design from $12/m². For a 150 m² house: the shell from 150 m² × $600 = $90,000, turnkey from 150 m² × $1,100 = $165,000, the design from 150 m² × $12 = $1,800. Landscape design of the plot costs from $50 per 100 m².

The total is affected by:

  • the type and cross-section of the timber
  • the floor area and number of storeys
  • the foundation for the soil on the plot
  • the roof and the number of windows
  • the heating and ventilation system
  • the class of finishes

The exact amount comes from the estimate based on the design. A comparison of solid timber with timber frame and aerated concrete is in the article what to build a house from.

How wood lasts a long time

A timber house likes dryness and ventilation. That is why the design includes wide roof overhangs, gutters, a plinth above ground level and ventilation, and the wood is treated with protective compounds. These things cost little at the construction stage and save on facade repairs later.

How we build

A timber house is built by our own team of permanent staff, from the design and foundation to the finishes. We build to our design or to yours, checking the drawings and details. Payment is split to suit you — 50/50, 50/30/20, 50/40/10 or several instalments, starting from a 10% advance. Photo reports come every 1–2 weeks, and an online camera 24/7 is available for an extra fee. One manager and a 12-month warranty on works.

Questions and answers

How much does a turnkey timber house cost?

From $1,100/m² with services and finishes; the shell from $600/m². For 150 m² turnkey — from $165,000. The exact amount comes from the estimate based on the design.

Which is better: glulam or profiled timber?

Glulam hardly settles, so finishing can start sooner, but it costs more. Profiled timber is cheaper but needs time to settle. The choice depends on the budget and the timeline.

When can finishing start?

With glulam — after the thermal envelope. With profiled timber — after the settling pause, the length of which we state in the works schedule.

Do you build timber houses outside Kyiv?

Yes. We build in all regions of Ukraine and in Europe.
