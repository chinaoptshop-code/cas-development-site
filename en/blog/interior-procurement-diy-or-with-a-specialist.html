Interior procurement: do it yourself or hire a specialist, and how to save

CAS Development · 1 November 2026

Interior procurement is everything that turns a renovated apartment or house into a home: furniture, lighting, textiles, decor, appliances. You can take it on yourself or hand it to a procurement specialist. We explain what the work consists of, when buying yourself really saves money and when it turns into overpayments and delays.

What interior procurement is

Procurement is selecting, ordering, delivering and installing everything that fills the space after renovation. It sounds like “just buying furniture”, but in practice it means dozens of items, and in a house hundreds: from the sofa and kitchen to sockets, curtain rails, hooks and bulbs of the right colour temperature. Each has dimensions, production time, delivery terms and a place in the schedule.

A procurement specialist is a person or team who runs this process from the designer's specification to the moment the last vase is on the shelf. In practice the same studio that did the design and renovation often takes it on, so decisions do not get lost between contractors. It matters that the specialist works from the project specification rather than personal taste: the job is to reproduce the concept precisely within the agreed budget.

What procurement includes

  • cabinet and custom furniture: kitchen, wardrobes, walk-in closets, bathroom vanities
  • upholstered furniture: sofas, armchairs, beds, mattresses
  • lighting: chandeliers, wall lights, track systems, accent lighting, bulbs of the right temperature
  • sanitaryware and accessories, if not included in the renovation estimate
  • household appliances, built-in and freestanding
  • textiles: curtains, rails, rugs, bedding, towels
  • decor: mirrors, pictures, vases, plants

What a procurement specialist does: the stages

  1. Checks the design project specification against the budget and suggests alternatives where appropriate.
  2. Gathers supplier offers and compares prices, lead times and delivery terms.
  3. Agrees samples with you: fabrics, finishes, colours, hardware.
  4. Places orders and tracks production of long-lead items.
  5. Coordinates deliveries with the renovation schedule so nothing sits in dust or waits for months.
  6. Receives goods, checks completeness and defects, arranges returns and replacements.
  7. Organises assembly, installation and placement according to the furniture plan.

Custom furniture is separate from purchasing. Custom kitchens and wardrobes are designed together with the services and made after the check measurement, once finishing works are complete. The specialist brings these two lines — ready-made furniture and bespoke production — into one schedule. If the kitchen is ordered earlier, any deviation of walls or floor from the drawings has to be compensated during installation.

Turnkey procurement

Doing procurement yourself: when it pays off

Buying yourself is a normal choice if you understand the scope and are ready to spend time on it. It works best in these situations:

  • a small apartment with a simple interior and mostly ready-made furniture
  • you have a clear specification with dimensions from the designer
  • you are not pressed for time and can wait for the right item
  • you are ready to receive deliveries, check defects and arrange assembly yourself
  • a large part of the items already exist or are moving from your old home

The main saving of buying yourself is your time, which you “pay” instead of a specialist. The main risk is mistakes in dimensions and timing: a sofa that will not fit through the door, a chandelier that does not suit the ceiling height, or a kitchen ordered before the walls were ready. So if you choose this route, ask the designer for a specification with exact dimensions and acceptable alternatives — it is prepared as part of the apartment design project.

How not to get the dimensions wrong

  • measure not only the room but also doors, the lift and stairwells — the route the furniture will take
  • check the height of chandeliers and pendants against the ceiling height and the table below
  • check that wardrobe doors and drawers open without hitting the bed or table
  • confirm real dimensions including legs, armrests and fold-out mechanisms
  • for built-in appliances look at installation dimensions, not the overall size on the product card

When to hand procurement to a team

  • the property is large: a house, a penthouse, several apartments at once
  • many custom items with different production times
  • you live in another city or abroad and cannot receive deliveries
  • you need to meet a specific move-in or letting date
  • the interior is complex: scene lighting, built-in furniture, stone, brass, veneer
  • it matters to you that the result matches the visualisations in detail

In such cases turnkey procurement takes the coordination of dozens of suppliers off your hands. You approve decisions and the budget, and the team is responsible for everything arriving on time, without defects, and ending up in its place. This is especially noticeable for rental homes: the apartment earns from the day the first tenant moves in, so every week of waiting for furniture is lost income.

Comparison: yourself or with a specialist

  • Time: yourself — your evenings and weekends on searching, comparing and deliveries; with a specialist — approvals only
  • Item prices: yourself — retail prices and promotions; with a team — access to suppliers and manufacturers
  • Risk of dimension mistakes: yourself — on you; with a team — checked against the drawings
  • Defects and returns: yourself — you handle the correspondence; with a team — the contractor handles receiving and replacements
  • Schedule: yourself — easy to fall out of step with the renovation; with a team — deliveries tied to stages
  • Interior integrity: yourself — depends on discipline; with a team — compliance with the project is controlled

How to save on procurement

Saving on procurement rarely means buying the cheapest. The right priorities and order of purchases give far more.

  • split the budget into accents and background: expensive items go where they are seen and touched every day
  • do not buy furniture before the layout is final — the most expensive mistake is an item that does not fit
  • order long-lead items in advance so you do not overpay for urgent replacements
  • combine orders with one supplier where possible — it simplifies delivery and warranty
  • for repeated items — chairs, lights, textiles for several apartments — consider batches directly from the factory
  • leave decor for last: it is easier to choose when the space is already lived in

Another line of saving is the schedule. Items ordered at the last moment often have to be replaced with whatever is in stock, and urgent delivery and repeat visits by installers cost extra. When purchases are planned together with the renovation, you choose from the full range, not from what can arrive in time.

The point about batches matters especially for owners of several rental apartments, apart-hotels or offices. For such properties CAS arranges batch orders of furniture, lighting and textiles directly from factories in China, with delivery in 35–70 days and made-to-order options for colour, size and material.

What procurement costs

The procurement budget has two parts: the cost of the items themselves and the work of selecting, purchasing, logistics and installation. At CAS Development apartment furnishing starts from $250/m². If some furniture already exists or is moving with you, the budget falls proportionally — this is visible in the specification before purchasing starts.

We wrote in detail about the order of purchases — what to buy first and what last — in the article “Turnkey apartment furnishing: order and budget”.

Checklist before procurement starts

  • there is an approved furniture plan with dimensions and passages
  • there is a specification: items, quantities, dimensions, finishes, budget
  • it is decided what will be custom-made and what ready-made
  • the end date of finishing works and the desired move-in date are known
  • it is agreed who receives deliveries and checks for defects
  • there is temporary storage space in case items arrive early
  • it is decided who assembles and installs the furniture and lighting

If at least half of the points have no answer yet, it is too early to start buying: first close the layout and specification. This is a case where a few days of preparation save weeks of waiting and money on returns.

Questions and answers

What does interior procurement include?

Selecting, ordering, delivering and installing furniture, lighting, textiles, decor and appliances to the project specification, plus receiving goods and replacing defective items.

Is it cheaper to furnish an apartment yourself?

You can save on the service, but you spend your own time and take on the risks of mistakes in dimensions, timing and defects. For simple apartments this is justified; for complex properties rarely.

How much does apartment furnishing cost at CAS?

From $250/m². For a 75 m² apartment the guide figure is from $18,750; the exact sum depends on the specification.

Can furniture be ordered in batches directly from the factory?

Yes. For several rental apartments, apart-hotels or offices we arrange batch orders directly from factories in China with delivery in 35–70 days, including made-to-order items.

When should custom furniture be ordered?

Design it together with the services; produce it after the check measurement, when finishing works are complete.
