Aerated concrete or ceramic block: what to build your house from

CAS Development · 16 November 2026

Aerated concrete and ceramic block are the two most common materials for a masonry house in Ukraine. Both give warm, durable walls, but they behave differently in laying, finishing and fixing. We compare them in plain words so you can choose the material together with your architect rather than by the advertising.

Aerated concrete and ceramic block: what they are

Aerated concrete (AAC) is a cellular concrete: inside are thousands of tiny pores, so the block is light, warm and easy to cut with a saw. The blocks have precise dimensions, so they are laid on a thin layer of adhesive rather than a thick mortar joint.

Ceramic block is fired clay with cavities inside, often called “warm ceramics”. The blocks are large-format, with tongues and grooves on the sides, so the vertical joint is not filled with mortar, and the horizontal one is laid on mortar or a special foam — depending on the manufacturer's system.

Both materials suit a house of one or two storeys with an attic. The final choice of grade and wall thickness is made by the architect in the design — based on loads, the thermal calculation and the building location.

Strength and floor slabs

Ceramic block is usually stronger in compression, so it carries heavy cast-in-place floor slabs and point loads better. With aerated concrete, a reinforced concrete ring beam is cast under the slab to spread the load along the wall. Most designs include a ring beam for ceramics as well.

In practice the difference matters for houses with long spans, a heavy tiled roof or several storeys. For a typical one- or two-storey house both materials work, provided the structural engineer has calculated the details correctly.

Warmth, moisture and insulation

Both materials are warm, but neither replaces the thermal calculation. Whether extra facade insulation is needed, and how thick, depends on the wall thickness, the block grade and the region. An insulated wall is calculated as a build-up of layers — masonry, insulation, facade — and each layer has its role.

  • aerated concrete absorbs moisture more readily, so it is not left unfinished for long and the plinth is protected from water
  • ceramic is less sensitive to moisture, but the cavities in the block must not be left open at cut edges and under window sills
  • in both cases thermally broken window installation and lintels without cold bridges matter
  • facade materials are chosen to be vapour-permeable or properly ventilated so moisture can leave the wall outwards

We showed how the layers of wall, floor and roof are built up in the article on wall, floor and roof build-ups.

Laying, finishing and fixings

Aerated concrete is easier to work with: it is easy to cut and chase for wiring, and the even wall after laying needs a thinner coat of plaster. Ceramic block is cut with a special saw, and chases are made more carefully so as not to break the internal webs of the block.

With fixings it is the other way round: an ordinary plug holds poorly in both aerated concrete and hollow ceramic. For wall cabinets, a kitchen, a water heater or a TV, special anchors or embedded fixings are used, and the positions of heavy items are best marked in the design in advance. More on supervising the masonry in the article on building an aerated concrete house step by step.

Which to choose: a quick summary

  • aerated concrete — when laying speed, easy working and even walls for finishing matter
  • ceramic block — when you need higher strength, heavy floor slabs or lower sensitivity to moisture
  • in both cases — a design with a thermal calculation, a ring beam and well-detailed window junctions

Questions and answers

Which is warmer — aerated concrete or ceramic?

Both materials are warm. Whether extra insulation is needed, and how thick, is determined by the thermal calculation in the design for the specific wall and region.

Which is stronger?

Ceramic block is usually stronger in compression and carries heavy floor slabs better. With aerated concrete a ring beam under the slab is a must.

Can a kitchen and water heater be hung on aerated concrete?

Yes, but on special anchors or embedded fixings. Positions for heavy items are best marked in the design before the walls go up.
