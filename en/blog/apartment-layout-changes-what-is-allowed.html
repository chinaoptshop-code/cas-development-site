Changing an apartment layout: what you can and cannot change

CAS Development · 16 November 2026

Changing the layout is the cheapest way to get a completely different home within the same square metres. But not every wall can be removed and not every bathroom can be moved. We explain what is usually changed, where the limits lie, how to test the idea before the renovation and what the design and works cost.

What is most often changed in an apartment layout?

Most layout changes in Kyiv are not about tearing everything down but a few targeted changes that make the apartment more convenient for a particular family. Typical requests we get:

  • combine the kitchen and living room into one bright space
  • create a walk-in wardrobe instead of a storeroom or part of the hallway
  • add a second bathroom or enlarge the existing one
  • carve out a study or a child's room from the living room or hallway
  • remove long corridors that eat up square metres and light

In a new-build with an open-plan shell there are fewer such questions: the walls do not exist yet and can be built straight away where needed. In an older apartment you have to work with existing walls, risers and services, so there are fewer options and more careful checks are needed.

What can and cannot be changed in an apartment layout?

Non-load-bearing partitions can usually be moved, removed or added. The limits come from elements on which the whole building or the neighbours depend:

  • load-bearing walls and columns — openings in them are possible only to a structural engineer's calculation, and as a rule they cannot be removed completely
  • ventilation ducts and shafts — they cannot be reduced or moved
  • gas appliances — a kitchen with gas is not combined with a living room without the separation required by regulations
  • wet areas — a bathroom or kitchen is, as a rule, not moved above the neighbours' living rooms below
  • common property of the building — risers, shafts, part of the corridor outside the apartment door

That is why, before the renovation, we check the structure and services on site and show layout options that already take these limits into account. That way you choose from real solutions, not from pictures that would later have to be redone.

Where do the details matter most: kitchen, bathroom or wardrobe?

A combined kitchen-living room is the most popular request. If the wall is not load-bearing and there is no gas in the kitchen, or it can be separated according to regulations, a solution can usually be found. It is important to plan the extractor straight away: in an open space, kitchen smells drift into the living room, so the extractor's power and position go into the design.

Moving a bathroom or kitchen depends on where the risers are and on the fall of the drainage. The further the new point from the riser, the higher the floor has to be raised or a pump installed. Another condition is what lies below the apartment: as a rule, we do not move wet areas above the neighbours' living rooms.

A walk-in wardrobe is the simplest change: a plasterboard partition and well-planned lighting are enough. But its position depends on whether it would block a window, radiator or ventilation, so it too is drawn on the plan.

How do you test a layout idea before the renovation?

  1. Measure the apartment: walls, openings, heights, risers, ventilation, consumer unit.
  2. Check the structure: which walls are load-bearing, where the services run; if needed, a technical assessment of the structure.
  3. Layout options: 2–3 solutions with furniture placement, to compare not only square metres but how it will feel to live in the apartment.
  4. Choose an option and produce drawings: demolition, new partitions, relocation of electrics and plumbing.
  5. A renovation estimate based on these drawings — before work starts.

Whether official approval is required depends on the scope of the changes and the type of building. We prepare drawings and support — if needed, terms by individual arrangement. The outcome of the approval depends on the authorities, so we cannot guarantee it, and we say so honestly before starting.

Why change the layout together with the renovation?

It makes most sense to change the layout together with a full apartment renovation or in a new-build, while the walls are not yet finished. Then demolition, new partitions, relocation of electrics and plumbing, screed and waterproofing go in one cycle, and nothing has to be broken twice.

If you are not sure you need a full design project, or first just want to understand how to arrange the rooms, read the article on the space planning: it explains when that is enough.

How much does an apartment layout change cost?

Renovation starts from $550/m²: works and rough materials; finishing materials, furniture and appliances are costed separately in the estimate. The exact amount comes from the estimate based on the drawings; if CAS carries out the renovation, the final total stays within ±5% of it.

Questions and answers

Can the kitchen be combined with the living room?

Often yes, if the wall is not load-bearing and there is no gas in the kitchen or it can be separated according to regulations. We check the structure and services on site and offer options.

Can a bathroom be moved?

It depends on the risers, the drainage fall and what lies below the apartment. As a rule, we do not move wet areas above the neighbours' living rooms.

Do you help with approval of a layout change?

Yes, we prepare drawings and support — if needed, terms by individual arrangement. The outcome depends on the authorities and the scope of changes, so we do not guarantee it.
