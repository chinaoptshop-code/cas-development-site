Landscape from scratch — 16 stages of a garden

Scroll and the plot turns into a garden: from the survey to evening light. Choose the size and style, switch on the X-ray to see drainage, irrigation and cables under the lawn.

  1. Land surveyOur surveyor maps the terrain and boundaries, sets out the paths and terrace and fixes benchmarks.
  2. Drone surveyOur specialist films the plot from a drone: an orthophoto and heights become the base for the design on the real site.
  3. Design on the plotWe mark out the zones from the design — paths, terrace, lawn, beds and tree positions — before the first spade.
  4. GradingWe level the plot and set falls away from the house so water goes where it should.
  5. DrainageTrenches lined with geotextile, gravel and a perforated pipe falling to a soakaway: no standing water on the lawn.
  6. IrrigationA main line from the pump, rotor sprinklers for the lawn and drip lines for beds and shrubs.
  7. Lighting cablesCable in conduit at depth, with outlets for lights along the paths, by the terrace and the trees.
  8. EdgingEdging fixes the borders of paths and beds: pavers stay put and the lawn stays out of the flowers.
  9. PathsA compacted gravel base, geotextile, a sand-cement bed — then pavers laid stone by stone with staggered joints.
  10. TerraceJoists on adjustable pedestals with a fall, then composite or timber decking with even ventilation gaps.
  11. Soil and roll-out lawnTopsoil, rolling — then turf laid in staggered strips with tight seams.
  12. TreesA planting pit with drainage and good soil, staking and a mulched tree circle — the tree takes root.
  13. ShrubsShrub groups and hedges to the planting plan, with a drip line to every shrub.
  14. Flower bedsA drainage layer, fertile soil, perennials to a flowering plan and bark mulch on top.
  15. Relaxation areaA fire pit or gazebo on a prepared base, benches and light — a place you want to go out to in the evening.
  16. Lighting and handoverEvening light switches on in scenes; we hand over the garden with a certificate and show how to run irrigation and lights.

Layer build-ups

Path build-up
  1. Pavers or stone 60–80 mm — Laid with staggered joints, joints filled with sand
  2. Sand-cement bed 30–50 mm — Levels and holds every stone
  3. Crushed stone 150–200 mm — Load-bearing base compacted in layers
  4. Geotextile — — Keeps the gravel out of the soil
  5. Compacted subgrade — — Base with a fall for run-off
Lawn with drainage
  1. Roll-out turf 20–30 mm — Staggered strips, tight seams
  2. Topsoil 100–150 mm — Rolled, no clods
  3. Sand 50–100 mm — Levels and lets water through
  4. Drainage gravel with pipe 100–200 mm — Perforated pipe falling to a soakaway
  5. Geotextile — — Wraps the drain so it does not silt up
Terrace build-up
  1. Composite or timber decking 20–28 mm — Gaps for ventilation and run-off
  2. Joists 40×60 mm — Spacing per the board maker
  3. Adjustable pedestals — — Fall and level without concreting the whole area
  4. Crushed stone 100 mm — Firm free-draining base
  5. Geotextile — — Against weeds and settlement
Flower bed build-up
  1. Bark mulch 50–70 mm — Holds moisture, keeps weeds down
  2. Fertile soil 250–400 mm — For perennial roots
  3. Drainage 50–100 mm — Gravel or expanded clay on heavy soil
  4. Edging — — Keeps the line between bed and lawn
